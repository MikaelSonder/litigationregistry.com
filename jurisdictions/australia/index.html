Commercial disputes in Australia operate across parallel State and Territory court systems and the federal court system. Correct forum selection is often the first and most important strategic decision.

Large State-law commercial disputes commonly proceed in State or Territory Supreme Courts and their commercial lists, while disputes arising under Commonwealth law may fall within the Federal Court's broad federal commercial jurisdiction.

Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Australia is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, corporations, finance, trade, construction, insolvency, competition, consumer protection, intellectual property and other commercial activity. Australia has both State and Territory court systems and a federal court system, and the distinction between them is central to commercial dispute strategy.

Large commercial disputes governed by State or Territory law are commonly heard in the relevant Supreme Court, often through a commercial court or commercial list designed for complex business matters. The Federal Court of Australia has broad jurisdiction over civil matters arising under Commonwealth law and manages federal commercial and corporations disputes through its Commercial and Corporations National Practice Area.

For businesses, a commercially useful Australian litigation strategy requires early analysis of the federal element, the relevant State or Territory, contractual forum clauses, commercial-list suitability, evidence, interim relief, costs, appeal exposure and enforceability. The High Court of Australia is the apex court for final appeals from federal and State or Territory courts where the required appellate route is available.

Court Structure

Australia's commercial litigation environment combines State and Territory courts with federal courts. The correct forum depends on the cause of action, applicable law, parties, remedy, amount in dispute, location and whether a Commonwealth law forms part of the controversy.

State and Territory Supreme CourtsSuperior courts with unlimited civil jurisdiction in their respective jurisdictions. Major State-law commercial disputes are commonly heard here, often in specialist commercial lists or commercial courts.
State and Territory Lower CourtsLocal, Magistrates', District or County Courts hear civil matters within their monetary and statutory jurisdiction, with arrangements differing by State or Territory.
Federal Court of AustraliaNational superior court with broad civil jurisdiction over matters arising under Australian federal law, including corporations, competition, consumer protection, insolvency, financial and transactional disputes, intellectual property, taxation and commercial arbitration matters within federal jurisdiction.
Commercial and Corporations NPAThe Federal Court's Commercial and Corporations National Practice Area manages federal commercial and corporations disputes through specialist case management and practice arrangements.
Federal Circuit and Family CourtMay deal with less complex general federal law matters within its jurisdiction; it should be distinguished from the Federal Court's higher-level commercial and corporations practice.
High Court of AustraliaAustralia's apex court, exercising final appellate jurisdiction over qualifying appeals from State, Territory and federal courts, and original jurisdiction in defined constitutional and federal matters.
Procedure

Australian civil procedure is not governed by one nationwide code for all commercial disputes. The applicable rules depend on the court selected: each State and Territory has its own procedural rules, and the Federal Court applies its own rules, practice notes and National Practice Area framework. Commercial lists often provide focused case management for complex disputes.

Forum and CompetenceForum selection depends on the governing law, federal element, contractual forum clause, subject matter, parties, amount, territorial connection and available commercial-list route.
CommencementProceedings are generally commenced by filing an originating process, usually a writ or originating application, with the appropriate court registry and serving the initiating documents on each defendant.
State Commercial ListsState and Territory Supreme Courts commonly use commercial lists, commercial courts or dedicated case-management tracks to manage complex business disputes efficiently.
Federal ProcedureFederal Court commercial and corporations matters are managed under the Commercial and Corporations National Practice Area and related practice note framework.
Case ManagementAustralian commercial litigation typically involves active judicial case management, directions, evidence preparation, interlocutory applications, mediation and trial preparation.
Language and RepresentationProceedings are conducted in English. Complex commercial matters commonly involve solicitors, counsel, experts and coordinated business-side document management.
Process Flow
1. Dispute AssessmentIdentify the commercial conflict, legal position, business objective, governing law and likely counterparty response.
2. State or Federal Forum AnalysisDetermine whether the dispute belongs in a State or Territory court, the Federal Court, a commercial list, arbitration or another dispute-resolution route.
3. Evidence and Urgency ReviewOrganise contracts, correspondence, financial records, witness evidence, technical material and assess interim-relief or preservation needs.
4. CommencementFile the appropriate originating process and serve each defendant in accordance with the applicable court rules.
5. ProceedingsManage pleadings, discovery, evidence, directions, interlocutory applications, mediation and trial preparation.
6. Judgment and AppealAssess the result, State or federal appeal route and whether a further High Court appeal is legally and commercially justified.
7. EnforcementTranslate the result into payment, asset recovery, compliance or settlement leverage.
Appeals

Appeal routes depend on the court of first instance. State and Territory Supreme Court judgments are appealed through the relevant State or Territory appellate structure. Federal Court appeals are generally heard by the Full Court of the Federal Court. The High Court of Australia hears final appeals where special leave or another available route is granted.

State and Territory AppealsAppeals proceed through the relevant State or Territory Court of Appeal or appellate division, depending on the jurisdiction and originating court.
Federal Court AppealsAppeals from Federal Court first-instance judgments are generally heard by the Full Court of the Federal Court.
High Court ReviewThe High Court hears final appeals in qualifying matters, generally requiring special leave to appeal.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.

Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside merits analysis. The commercial value of an Australian judgment depends on asset availability, enforceability of the result and the practical route to payment or compliance in the relevant State, Territory or foreign jurisdiction.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, security, group structure, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Interim StrategyEarly interim relief may be material where there is a risk of asset dissipation, evidence loss or ineffective enforcement.
Cross-Border DimensionForeign judgment recognition, asset location, international service and applicable statutory or common-law rules can matter where the dispute extends beyond Australia.
Cross-Border Relevance

Australia is a major Asia-Pacific commercial jurisdiction with significant cross-border trade, finance, resources, infrastructure and technology activity. International disputes require careful coordination of court selection, governing law, service, evidence, interim relief, recognition and enforcement.

Forum SelectionCross-border contracts should assess State or federal court clauses, arbitration clauses, governing law, federal statutory elements, service arrangements, asset location and enforcement destination together.
Federal InterfaceFederal Court jurisdiction may be engaged where the controversy arises under Commonwealth law, including corporations, competition, consumer, insolvency, IP or taxation legislation.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the proper State or federal forum, service pathway, evidence, interim protection, authority documentation and enforcement destination early.
Practical RiskA valid claim can still lose commercial value if the wrong forum is selected, discovery is not managed, interim protection is delayed or enforcement has not been planned.
Operating Constraints & Risks
Forum RiskState, Territory and federal court pathways have different jurisdictional foundations, procedures, commercial lists and appellate routes.
Federal-Element RiskFederal Court jurisdiction requires a sufficient federal connection; a purely common-law contract dispute may not itself create federal jurisdiction.
Discovery and Cost RiskWeak document preservation, poor discovery planning or disproportionate interlocutory activity can increase cost and reduce commercial leverage.
Interim-Relief RiskDelay may reduce the practical availability or value of freezing, injunction and other urgent protective relief.
Recovery RiskA favourable decision may have limited commercial value where assets are unavailable or enforcement has not been planned.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in Australia?Large commercial disputes are commonly heard by the Supreme Courts of Australia’s states and territories, while the Federal Court hears commercial and corporations disputes within federal jurisdiction. Lower-value matters may proceed in State or Territory lower courts.
When Does the Federal Court of Australia Have Jurisdiction?The Federal Court has broad jurisdiction over civil matters arising under Australian federal law, including corporations, competition and consumer protection, insolvency, financial and transactional disputes, intellectual property, taxation and other federal matters.
Do Australian Courts Have Specialist Commercial Lists?Yes. Many State and Territory Supreme Courts use commercial lists or commercial courts to manage complex business disputes, while the Federal Court has a Commercial and Corporations National Practice Area for federal commercial and corporations matters.
What Is the Final Appellate Court in Australia?The High Court of Australia is Australia’s apex court and hears final appeals from State, Territory and federal courts where special leave or another appellate route is available.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Australia?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Australia, but should assess the correct State or federal forum, jurisdiction clause, service, evidence, discovery, interim protection and enforcement planning from the outset.
