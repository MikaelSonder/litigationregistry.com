Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Slovenia is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, payment obligations, corporate relationships, trade, insolvency-related activity or other professional transactions. The principal procedural framework is the Civil Procedure Act, commonly known as ZPP.

Slovenia’s ordinary court structure includes local courts, district courts, higher courts and the Supreme Court of the Republic of Slovenia. District courts have first-instance jurisdiction over commercial disputes. Commercial matters are therefore handled in the ordinary court structure through commercial departments rather than by separate standalone commercial courts.

For businesses, a commercially useful strategy requires early analysis of competence, evidence, procedural route, electronic communication, interim protection, appeal exposure and actual recoverability. The objective is not simply to obtain a decision, but to achieve an enforceable business result.

Court Structure

Slovenia’s ordinary court system consists of local courts, district courts, higher courts and the Supreme Court. Commercial matters are handled by district courts at first instance, usually through their commercial departments.

Local CourtsFirst-instance courts for matters allocated to them, including smaller civil claims and other matters under the Civil Procedure Act.
District CourtsFirst-instance courts for commercial disputes, insolvency and connected matters, intellectual property matters and relevant applications for interim measures.
Higher CourtsHear appeals against first-instance decisions of local and district courts.
Supreme Court of the Republic of SloveniaAt the apex of the ordinary court system; exercises functions assigned by law, including extraordinary review within the applicable procedural framework.
Commercial DepartmentsCommercial disputes are handled within the ordinary court system through commercial departments; Slovenia does not maintain separate standalone commercial courts.
Procedure

The Civil Procedure Act governs civil litigation. Articles 480 to 484 regulate the application of procedural rules to commercial disputes. A commercial dispute is generally one in which the parties fall within the legally defined professional or institutional categories, including companies, institutions, cooperatives, the state or self-governing local communities.

Forum and CompetenceDistrict courts have first-instance jurisdiction over commercial disputes. Territorial competence and any contractual forum clause should be assessed before filing.
CommencementProceedings commence through filing with the competent court. The available electronic route depends on the procedure and e-Sodstvo service design.
Pleadings and Case FramingThe claim and defence should establish the factual basis, legal grounds, relief sought and supporting evidence in a coherent court-ready form.
Commercial ProcedureCommercial dispute rules under ZPP apply to qualifying disputes and should be assessed from the start of the case.
Electronic CommunicationThe e-Sodstvo portal enables electronic services and electronic service under the Civil Procedure Act, subject to the relevant procedural and user requirements.

Appeals

Higher courts hear appeals against first-instance decisions of local and district courts. The Supreme Court is relevant to the extent an extraordinary review route or other statutory remedy is available.

Ordinary AppealAppeals from district-court commercial decisions are generally heard by the relevant higher court.
Supreme ReviewThe Supreme Court may be relevant through extraordinary review or other remedies available under the procedural framework.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.

Witness and Expert EvidenceWitness, technical, accounting or valuation evidence may be needed depending on the dispute and the issues in proof.
Interim ProtectionDistrict courts can decide applications for interim measures filed before the main dispute in matters that fall within commercial-dispute rules, arbitration-agreement matters and intellectual property matters.
Primary ReliefCommercial claims may seek payment, performance, damages, declaratory relief, protective measures or other available remedies.
Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside the merits analysis. The commercial value of a Slovenian judgment depends on the availability of assets, the enforceability of the result and the practical route to payment or compliance.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, security, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Electronic InterfaceElectronic filing is particularly relevant to enforcement procedures through the e-Sodstvo environment, subject to the applicable rules.
Cross-Border DimensionEU rules and applicable international instruments can matter where the judgment, debtor, assets or relevant business activity extends beyond Slovenia.
Cross-Border Relevance

Slovenia is an EU Member State. Cross-border commercial litigation may require analysis of EU rules on jurisdiction, service, evidence and recognition or enforcement, alongside Slovenian court competence and domestic procedure.

Forum SelectionCross-border cases should assess contractual jurisdiction clauses, the defendant’s establishment, place of performance, applicable EU rules and domestic competence rules.
European Union InterfaceEU judicial-cooperation rules can be central to civil and commercial disputes involving parties, evidence, assets or judgments in different Member States.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the proper forum, service pathway, translation and document requirements, potential interim protection and enforcement destination early.
Practical RiskA valid claim can still lose commercial value if it is filed in the wrong forum, poorly documented or unsupported by an enforcement plan.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in Slovenia?District courts have first-instance jurisdiction over commercial disputes in Slovenia. Higher courts hear appeals, while the Supreme Court of the Republic of Slovenia is at the apex of the ordinary court system.
What Is the Main Procedural Framework for Slovenian Commercial Litigation?The Civil Procedure Act, known as ZPP, is the principal procedural framework. Articles 480 to 484 govern the application of rules on commercial disputes.
Are Commercial Disputes Handled by Separate Commercial Courts?Slovenia does not have separate standalone commercial courts. District courts and their commercial departments handle commercial matters at first instance.
Can Court Documents Be Filed Electronically in Slovenia?The e-Sodstvo portal supports electronic services in civil proceedings. The availability and mandatory use of electronic filing or service depend on the procedure and the user category.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Slovenia?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Slovenia, but should assess forum, service, evidence, document formalities, translation, electronic communication and enforcement planning from the outset.
