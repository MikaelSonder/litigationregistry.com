Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Qatar is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, trade, finance, construction, real estate, corporate relationships, banking, investment and other commercial activity. Qatar has a national court system governed principally by Law No. 13 of 1990 promulgating the Civil and Commercial Procedure Law, alongside a specialist civil and commercial court within the Qatar Financial Centre.

The national court system includes Courts of First Instance, a Court of Appeal and a Court of Cassation. First-instance jurisdiction is allocated between summary or partial circuits and plenary circuits based on the value and nature of the claim. Arabic is the language of the national courts. The Qatar International Court, forming part of the Qatar International Court and Dispute Resolution Centre, operates within the Qatar Financial Centre and has First Instance and Appellate Circuits for qualifying QFC-related civil and commercial disputes.

For businesses, the central strategic issue is forum selection: national court, Qatar International Court, arbitration or another route. A commercially useful Qatar strategy requires early assessment of the QFC connection, contractual jurisdiction clause, language, authority documents, evidence, interim relief, appeal exposure, assets and enforcement destination.

Court Structure

Qatar's litigation environment includes the national civil and commercial court hierarchy and the separate Qatar International Court within the Qatar Financial Centre. Their jurisdiction, language and procedure differ materially.

National Courts of First InstanceNational civil and commercial cases are heard at first instance through Partial or Summary Circuits and Plenary Circuits according to the value and nature of the claim under the Civil and Commercial Procedure Law.
Partial or Summary CircuitA one-judge Court of First Instance circuit with jurisdiction over civil, commercial and administrative-contract disputes not exceeding the statutory value threshold specified in the Civil and Commercial Procedure Law.
Plenary CircuitHears civil and commercial claims falling outside the Partial Circuit's jurisdiction and matters assigned by law.
Court of AppealReviews eligible first-instance national court judgments under the applicable procedural framework.
Court of CassationNational final court of legal review for qualifying appellate judgments and decisions.
Qatar International CourtSpecialist civil and commercial court within the Qatar Financial Centre, comprising First Instance and Appellate Circuits. It hears qualifying QFC-related disputes under the QFC Law and its procedural rules.
QFC Regulatory TribunalSpecialist tribunal within the QICDRC structure that hears appeals from decisions of QFC institutions and regulators, distinct from the Court's civil and commercial jurisdiction.
Procedure

Law No. 13 of 1990, the Civil and Commercial Procedure Law, is the principal national procedural framework. It governs jurisdiction, court procedure, appeals and execution. Qatar International Court proceedings are governed by the QFC Law and the Court's regulations and procedural rules. The selected forum materially affects language, procedure, legal representation, evidence and enforcement strategy.

National Court CompetenceNational jurisdiction in commercial matters can be linked to the defendant's domicile, the place where the agreement was made or performed, or where performance should have occurred, subject to the Civil and Commercial Procedure Law.
National CommencementProceedings are commenced by filing the appropriate claim before the competent national court circuit, with the required pleadings, documents and procedural formalities.
National Court LanguageArabic is the language of the national courts. Foreign parties should plan for Arabic translations, authority documents and document formalities from the outset.
Qatar International Court JurisdictionThe Court hears qualifying civil and commercial disputes arising from QFC transactions, arrangements and relationships, including defined disputes between QFC entities, QFC institutions, contractors, employees and certain non-QFC parties, unless another dispute-resolution route is agreed.
Qatar International Court LanguageProceedings before the Qatar International Court may be conducted in English or Arabic in accordance with its rules and directions.
Arbitration InterfaceArbitration is a significant contractual alternative. Qatar's arbitration law treats an economic legal relationship, contractual or otherwise, as commercial in relevant circumstances.

Appeals

Appeal routes depend on the forum selected. National court cases generally move from the Court of First Instance to the Court of Appeal and, where permitted, to the Court of Cassation. Qatar International Court cases move from the First Instance Circuit to the Appellate Circuit under the Court's rules.

National AppealEligible first-instance national court judgments may be appealed to the Court of Appeal in accordance with the Civil and Commercial Procedure Law.
National CassationThe Court of Cassation reviews qualifying appellate decisions on legal and procedural grounds under the applicable national framework.
Qatar International Court AppealAppeals from First Instance Circuit decisions are heard by the Appellate Circuit in accordance with QICDRC procedural rules.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.

Language and TranslationNational court proceedings require Arabic-language readiness. Qatar International Court proceedings may be conducted in English or Arabic. The selected forum determines translation and formal-document requirements.
Witness and Expert EvidenceWitness, technical, accounting, valuation, financial or foreign-law evidence may be needed depending on the dispute and the selected forum.
Interim ProtectionUrgent measures may be relevant where delay risks assets, evidence, contractual rights or effective recovery. Available relief and standards depend on the selected court system.
Primary ReliefCommercial claims may seek payment, performance, damages, declarations, protective measures or other remedies available in the selected forum.
Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside merits analysis. In Qatar, the commercial value of a judgment depends on asset availability, the originating forum, the intended enforcement route and any need for national, QFC or international recognition and execution.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, company structure, security, bank exposure, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
National ExecutionThe Civil and Commercial Procedure Law includes provisions on enforcement of judgments, orders and official foreign documents, subject to the applicable national execution framework.
QFC InterfaceEnforcement between the Qatar International Court and national courts requires careful analysis of the applicable QFC, national and procedural mechanisms.
Cross-Border DimensionForeign judgment recognition, arbitral award enforcement, asset location, international service and applicable treaties or reciprocity can matter where the dispute extends beyond Qatar.
Cross-Border Relevance

Qatar is a significant regional commercial, investment, finance, infrastructure and energy jurisdiction. The coexistence of national courts and the Qatar International Court makes court selection, procedural language and contractual jurisdiction clauses especially important in cross-border disputes.

Forum SelectionCross-border contracts should assess Qatar national court clauses, Qatar International Court clauses, QFC connection, arbitration clauses, governing law, service arrangements, asset location and enforcement destination together.
Qatar International Court OptionThe Court's jurisdiction includes defined QFC-related civil and commercial disputes. Parties should assess whether the QFC connection and jurisdictional criteria are satisfied.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the correct Qatar forum, Arabic or English procedure, service pathway, authority documentation, evidence, interim protection and enforcement destination early.
Arbitration InterfaceArbitration may be an important contractual route for cross-border commercial disputes. Its relationship to national or QFC court jurisdiction and enforcement should be assessed from the outset.
Practical RiskA jurisdiction or arbitration clause is commercially useful only when it aligns with the parties, contract, QFC connection, procedural language, evidence, assets and realistic enforcement route.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in Qatar?Commercial disputes may be heard by Qatar’s national civil and commercial courts or, for disputes within the Qatar Financial Centre jurisdiction, by the Qatar International Court. The correct forum depends on the parties, contract, subject matter, QFC connection and jurisdiction agreement.
What Is the Main Procedural Law for Qatari Commercial Litigation?Law No. 13 of 1990 promulgating the Civil and Commercial Procedure Law provides the principal national procedural framework for civil and commercial litigation, including jurisdiction, appeals and execution.
What Is the Qatar International Court?The Qatar International Court is the civil and commercial court of the Qatar Financial Centre. It has First Instance and Appellate Circuits and hears qualifying QFC-related civil and commercial disputes under the QFC Law and its procedural rules.
Can Proceedings Be Conducted in English in Qatar?Arabic is the language of Qatar’s national courts. The Qatar International Court permits proceedings in English or Arabic, subject to its rules and directions.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Qatar?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Qatar, but should assess national court versus Qatar International Court jurisdiction, contractual clauses, Arabic or English procedure, authority documents, evidence, interim protection and enforcement planning from the outset.
