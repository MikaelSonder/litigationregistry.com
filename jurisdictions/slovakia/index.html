Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Slovakia is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, payment obligations, corporate relationships, trade or other professional activity. The main procedural framework is Act No. 160/2015 Coll., the Code of Civil Dispute Procedure.

Slovakia’s ordinary court system operates through district courts, regional courts and the Supreme Court of the Slovak Republic. District courts generally act as courts of first instance in civil and commercial cases, while regional courts generally hear appeals from district-court decisions. Specific rules may allocate defined categories of commercial disputes to a particular court with national competence.

For businesses, a useful litigation strategy requires early analysis of competence, the defendant’s registered seat, evidential readiness, digital filing conditions, interim protection, recovery prospects and the commercial value of an appeal.

Court Structure

Slovakia has a three-level ordinary court structure: district courts, regional courts and the Supreme Court of the Slovak Republic. District courts generally act at first instance in civil and commercial cases, unless procedural rules provide otherwise.

District CourtsGenerally act as courts of first instance in civil and commercial matters, unless a special competence rule applies.
Regional CourtsGenerally hear appeals against first-instance decisions of district courts; in exceptional cases they may act at first instance.
Supreme Court of the Slovak RepublicSits at the apex of the ordinary court system and has jurisdiction in ordinary and extraordinary appeals and other matters assigned by law.
Special CompetenceDefined commercial categories may be assigned to a specific court with nationwide territorial competence, including certain exchange-trade and public-procurement contract disputes.
Commercial RegisterCommercial register matters follow their own procedural and institutional routes and should be distinguished from ordinary commercial litigation.
Procedure

The Code of Civil Dispute Procedure regulates the conduct of the court, parties and other persons involved in the hearing and determination of disputes. The ordinary court of the defendant is the starting point for competence unless another rule applies.

Forum and CompetenceFor a legal entity, the ordinary court is generally the court in whose district it has its registered seat; for a foreign legal entity, the relevant organisational unit may be decisive.
CommencementClaims may be lodged in paper form or electronically. Electronic merits submissions must meet the applicable e-government authorisation requirements.
Pleadings and Case FramingThe claim and defence should establish the factual basis, legal grounds, relief sought and supporting evidence in a coherent court-ready form.
ServiceService rules include delivery during hearings or other court acts and delivery to a functional electronic mailbox where applicable.
RepresentationRepresentation by a lawyer is not universally mandatory in Slovak civil procedure, though complex commercial disputes typically require specialist legal management.
Process Flow
1. Dispute AssessmentIdentify the commercial conflict, legal position, business objective and likely counterparty response.
2. Forum AnalysisDetermine the competent Slovak court by subject matter, defendant location, special competence rules and any cross-border element.
3. Evidence ReviewOrganise contracts, invoices, correspondence, corporate records, technical material and financial support.
4. Claim or DefencePrepare the procedural submission, requested relief and legal basis in a coherent court-ready form.
5. ProceedingsManage service, pleadings, evidence, hearings, settlement considerations and procedural applications where relevant.
6. Judgment and AppealAssess the result, applicable appeal route and whether further review is commercially justified.
7. EnforcementTranslate the result into payment, compliance, asset recovery or settlement leverage.
Appeals

Regional courts generally act as courts of second instance for appeals against district-court decisions. The Supreme Court may be relevant for ordinary or extraordinary appeals and other review mechanisms assigned by law.

Ordinary AppealAppeals from district-court decisions are generally heard by the relevant regional court.
Supreme ReviewThe Supreme Court can hear ordinary and extraordinary appeals under the procedural framework and in the circumstances prescribed by law.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.
Evidence & Remedies
Documentary EvidenceContracts, amendments, orders, invoices, delivery records, notices, correspondence, corporate materials and payment records are commonly central.
Witness and Expert EvidenceWitness, technical, accounting or valuation evidence may be needed depending on the dispute and the issues in proof.
Interim ProtectionUrgent protective measures may be relevant where delay risks assets, evidence, contractual rights or effective recovery.
Primary ReliefCommercial claims may seek payment, performance, damages, declaratory relief, protective measures or other available remedies.
Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside the merits analysis. The commercial value of a Slovak judgment depends on the availability of assets, the enforceability of the result and the practical route to payment or compliance.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, security, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Cross-Border DimensionEU rules and applicable international instruments can matter where the judgment, debtor, assets or relevant business activity extends beyond Slovakia.
Cross-Border Relevance

Slovakia is an EU Member State. Cross-border commercial litigation may require analysis of EU rules on jurisdiction, service, evidence and recognition or enforcement, alongside Slovak court competence and domestic procedure.

Forum SelectionCross-border cases should assess contractual jurisdiction clauses, the defendant’s establishment, place of performance, applicable EU rules and domestic competence rules.
European Union InterfaceEU judicial-cooperation rules can be central to civil and commercial disputes involving parties, evidence, assets or judgments in different Member States.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the proper forum, service pathway, translation and document requirements, potential interim protection and enforcement destination early.
Practical RiskA valid claim can still lose commercial value if it is filed in the wrong forum, poorly documented or unsupported by an enforcement plan.
Operating Constraints & Risks

Electronic Filing RiskElectronic filing must meet the applicable technical, authentication and authorisation requirements.
Documentation RiskWeak contractual, payment or communication records can materially reduce litigation leverage.
Timing RiskDelay can affect evidence preservation, recovery prospects and settlement pressure.
Recovery RiskA favourable decision may have limited commercial value where assets are unavailable or enforcement has not been planned.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in Slovakia?District courts generally hear civil cases at first instance, including commercial disputes unless a rule provides otherwise. Regional courts generally hear appeals from district-court decisions, and the Supreme Court sits at the apex of the ordinary court system.
What Is the Main Procedural Framework?Act No. 160/2015 Coll., the Code of Civil Dispute Procedure, regulates the conduct of the court, parties and other persons involved in the hearing and determination of disputes.
Where Is a Commercial Claim Normally Filed?The ordinary court of a legal entity in commercial matters is generally the court in whose district the defendant has its registered seat, subject to the applicable jurisdiction rules and special competence provisions.
Can Commercial Litigation Be Initiated Electronically?Slovakia provides online services for filing petitions and submissions in district and regional courts in civil and commercial matters, subject to the applicable e-government authentication requirements.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Slovakia?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Slovakia, but should assess forum, service, evidence, document formalities, translation, electronic filing and enforcement planning from the outset.
