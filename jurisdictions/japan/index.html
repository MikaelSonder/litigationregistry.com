Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Japan is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, payment obligations, corporate relationships, trade, finance, intellectual property-adjacent activity and other professional transactions. The main procedural framework is the Code of Civil Procedure, Act No. 109 of 1996, supplemented by the Rules of Civil Procedure.

Japan has a uniform judicial system with the Supreme Court as its sole highest court. Commercial disputes do not generally proceed in separate commercial courts. Most substantial commercial claims are heard at first instance by district courts, with high courts acting as the primary appellate courts and the Supreme Court hearing final appeals on limited legal grounds. Summary courts handle lower-value civil claims within their statutory jurisdiction.

For businesses, a useful Japanese litigation strategy depends on early preparation of the factual and documentary record, a clear theory of the case, correct court selection, Japanese-language litigation readiness, appeal planning and realistic consideration of recovery. Japan has no civil jury system; professional judges assess the facts, apply the law and issue judgments.

Court Structure

Japan’s civil court structure includes summary courts, district courts, high courts and the Supreme Court. There is no separate general commercial court. Commercial claims are handled by ordinary civil courts, while particular major district courts may have divisions specialising in selected categories of commercial, corporate, intellectual property or insolvency matters.

Summary CourtsFirst-instance courts for civil claims within the statutory monetary jurisdiction, generally claims of JPY 1.4 million or less.
District CourtsPrincipal first-instance courts for substantial commercial and civil claims, including claims exceeding the summary court threshold and designated matters such as real-estate disputes.
High CourtsPrimary courts of second instance for appeals from district courts. High courts sit in major cities and operate through civil divisions.
Supreme CourtJapan’s sole highest court and final court of appeal. It focuses on limited legal, constitutional and prescribed procedural grounds rather than a new factual hearing.
SpecialisationTokyo and Osaka District Courts have divisions with subject-matter specialisation. The Intellectual Property High Court is a specialised branch of the Tokyo High Court for relevant IP appeals.
Procedure

The Code of Civil Procedure governs civil suits. An action is generally subject to the jurisdiction of the court having jurisdiction over the defendant’s general venue, subject to specific venue, contractual jurisdiction and international jurisdiction rules. A civil action begins with filing a complaint, followed by service through the court.

Forum and CompetenceThe defendant’s general venue is a central starting point. For corporations and other entities, venue is generally connected to the principal office or business office, subject to applicable special rules.
CommencementA civil action commences by filing a complaint with the competent court. The court then serves the complaint and summons on the defendant.
Pleadings and Case FramingThe claim should establish the factual basis, legal grounds, relief sought and supporting evidence in a coherent court-ready form.
LanguageJapanese is the practical language of litigation. Foreign parties should plan early for translations, document preparation, powers of attorney and local representation.
RepresentationLegal representation is not universally mandatory in ordinary civil litigation, but complex commercial disputes typically require experienced Japanese counsel and coordinated business-side preparation.
CostsA court filing fee is generally payable upon filing. Under the Code of Civil Procedure, court costs are generally borne by the losing party, subject to the applicable rules and decisions.

Appeals

A district-court decision may generally be appealed to the relevant high court. A final appeal from a high-court judgment to the Supreme Court is limited to prescribed legal and constitutional grounds; the Supreme Court does not ordinarily re-examine the high court’s factual findings.

Summary Court RouteFor claims originating before a summary court, the first appeal generally proceeds to a district court and a further appeal may proceed to a high court under the applicable rules.
District Court RouteFor most substantial commercial disputes, an appeal from the district court proceeds to the relevant high court.
Final AppealThe Supreme Court hears final appeals from high-court judgments when statutory grounds are met, focusing principally on legal interpretation and constitutional or procedural issues.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.
Evidence & Remedies
Documentary EvidenceContracts, amendments, orders, invoices, delivery records, notices, correspondence, corporate materials and payment records are commonly central.
Witness and Expert EvidenceWitness, technical, accounting or valuation evidence may be needed depending on the dispute and the issues in proof.
Document DisciplineCommercial litigation in Japan places high practical value on a clear, contemporaneous and well-organised documentary record.
Interim ProtectionProvisional remedies may be relevant where delay risks assets, evidence, contractual rights or effective recovery, subject to the applicable procedural requirements.
Primary ReliefCommercial claims may seek payment, performance, damages, declaratory relief, protective measures or other available remedies.
Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside merits analysis. The commercial value of a Japanese judgment depends on the availability of assets, enforceability of the result and the practical route to payment or compliance.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, security, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Corporate RecordFor Japanese corporate parties, corporate registration information may be needed to establish existence, head-office address and representative authority.
Cross-Border DimensionForeign-judgment recognition, international service, asset location and applicable treaties or domestic rules can matter where the dispute extends beyond Japan.
Cross-Border Relevance

Japan is a major international commercial jurisdiction. Cross-border business disputes require early consideration of jurisdiction clauses, defendant venue, service, Japanese-language evidence, translation, foreign parties, asset location and potential recognition or enforcement in other jurisdictions.

Forum SelectionCross-border cases should assess contractual jurisdiction clauses, the defendant’s business location, place of performance, statutory international jurisdiction and any arbitration agreement.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the proper court, service pathway, Japanese-language and translation requirements, authority documentation, potential interim protection and enforcement destination early.
International JurisdictionThe Code of Civil Procedure contains international jurisdiction rules for actions connected to persons, corporations and offices located in Japan.
Practical RiskA legally valid claim can still lose commercial value if the documentation is not litigation-ready in Japanese, the forum is poorly selected or enforcement has not been planned.
Operating Constraints & Risks
Forum RiskVenue, subject matter, amount in dispute and international jurisdiction rules can affect the proper court and procedural route.
Language RiskJapanese-language pleadings, evidence and translations require early planning and reliable document management.
Evidence RiskWeak documentary records can materially reduce litigation leverage in a system where written business evidence is highly significant.
Appeal RiskSupreme Court review is limited and should not be treated as a routine second factual review after a high-court decision.
Recovery RiskA favourable decision may have limited commercial value where assets are unavailable or enforcement has not been planned.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in Japan?Most commercial claims are heard at first instance by district courts. Summary courts hear lower-value claims, while high courts hear appeals from district courts and the Supreme Court is the final court of appeal.
Does Japan Have Separate Commercial Courts?Japan does not have standalone commercial courts. Commercial disputes are generally handled within the ordinary civil court system, although major district courts may have specialist divisions for particular subject areas.
What Is the Main Procedural Framework?The Code of Civil Procedure, Act No. 109 of 1996, is the principal procedural framework for civil litigation in Japan, supplemented by the Rules of Civil Procedure.
What Is the Appeal Structure for Commercial Cases in Japan?A district-court judgment is generally appealed to the relevant high court. A final appeal to the Supreme Court is limited to prescribed legal and constitutional grounds rather than a fresh review of factual findings.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Japan?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Japan, but should assess forum, service, Japanese-language documentation, evidence, authority documents, translations, timing and enforcement planning from the outset.
