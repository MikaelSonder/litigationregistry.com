Commercial disputes in Scotland may be heard in the Court of Session, Scotland's supreme civil court, including through its specialist Commercial Court procedure, or in Sheriff Courts where the relevant jurisdiction and commercial procedure apply.

Scottish commercial actions are designed to enable specialist judges to handle a broad range of business disputes quickly and flexibly. Correct court selection, Chapter 47 procedure, evidence, interim protection and enforcement planning are central from the outset.

Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Scotland is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, banking, insurance, trade, goods and services, construction, partnership, professional negligence, business property, technology, energy and other commercial activity. Scotland is a separate legal jurisdiction within the United Kingdom, with its own courts, procedure and legal tradition.

The Court of Session in Edinburgh is Scotland's supreme civil court. It sits as the Outer House at first instance and the Inner House on appeal. The Court of Session Commercial Court provides special procedure for commercial actions, enabling nominated commercial judges to manage a broad range of business disputes quickly and flexibly. Sheriff Courts also deal with commercial actions and may be the appropriate forum depending on value, complexity, location and procedural route.

For businesses, a commercially useful Scottish litigation strategy requires early analysis of Court of Session versus Sheriff Court forum, the suitability of Chapter 47 commercial-action procedure, jurisdiction clause, evidence, interim protection, Scottish expenses, appeal route and enforceability. Scotland's common-law and civil-law influences, distinct procedural terminology and separate legal system make it important not to treat Scotland as an extension of England and Wales.

Court Structure

Scotland's civil court system includes the Sheriff Courts, Sheriff Appeal Court and the Court of Session. The Court of Session is Scotland's supreme civil court and sits exclusively in Edinburgh. It has a first-instance Outer House and an appellate Inner House.

Sheriff CourtsLocal civil courts across Scotland that hear a broad range of civil and commercial matters. Commercial procedure may be available in relevant Sheriff Courts, depending on the case and court.
Sheriff Appeal CourtHears designated civil appeals from Sheriff Courts, subject to the applicable statutory and procedural route.
Court of Session Outer HouseFirst-instance division of Scotland's supreme civil court. Major, complex and specialist commercial actions may be raised in the Outer House and handled through the Commercial Court procedure.
Court of Session Inner HouseAppellate division of the Court of Session, reviewing decisions of the Outer House, Sheriff Courts and other bodies under the applicable route.
Commercial CourtSpecialist commercial-action procedure within the Outer House, with nominated commercial judges handling commercial cases on the Commercial Roll.
UK Supreme CourtFinal appellate court in selected Scottish civil cases, generally where permission is granted and the matter raises an arguable point of law of general public importance.
Court ChoiceThe Court of Session and Sheriff Courts have concurrent jurisdiction in many higher-value civil matters. Court choice should be assessed by value, complexity, urgency, specialist procedure, location, costs and commercial objective.
Commercial Court

The Court of Session Commercial Court is a specialist procedure rather than a separate standalone court. Chapter 47 of the Rules of the Court of Session applies to commercial actions and allows commercial judges to deal with business disputes quickly and flexibly. The definition of a commercial action is intentionally broad.

Commercial Action DefinitionA commercial action arises out of, or concerns, a transaction or dispute of a commercial or business nature, whether contractual or not, where the procedure is elected or the action is transferred into the commercial procedure.
Typical MattersCommercial and mercantile documents, sale or hire purchase of goods, import or export, carriage by land, air or sea, insurance, banking, financial services, commercial leases, building contracts, partnership disputes, professional negligence and business property.
Election of ProcedureThe pursuer may elect Chapter 47 commercial procedure when raising an appropriate Outer House action. An existing action may also be transferred to commercial-action procedure under the applicable rule.
Commercial RollOuter House commercial actions are heard and determined on the Commercial Roll by a judge nominated by the Lord President as a commercial judge, where available.
Specialist ManagementCommercial judges use preliminary hearings, procedural hearings, focused timetables, flexible orders and active case management to progress commercial disputes efficiently.
Electronic AdministrationMany Commercial Court documents and procedural communications can be lodged or managed through the court's designated electronic and email processes, subject to current court rules and directions.
Procedure

Scottish commercial procedure is governed by the Rules of the Court of Session, including Chapter 47 for commercial actions, court practice notes and procedural directions. Commercial actions are intended to be handled in a focused, flexible and efficient manner, with specialist judicial oversight from an early stage.

Forum and JurisdictionCorrect Scottish court selection depends on the contract, jurisdiction clause, value, complexity, location, type of relief, specialist procedure and whether Court of Session or Sheriff Court jurisdiction is appropriate.
CommencementOuter House proceedings are generally commenced by summons. A pursuer may elect commercial-action procedure in an appropriate Court of Session action under Chapter 47.
PleadingsScottish pleadings must present the parties' averments, legal basis and remedies sought in accordance with the applicable court rules and commercial-action procedure.
Preliminary HearingCommercial actions use preliminary hearings to identify issues, resolve procedural matters, define timetables and consider the appropriate future conduct of the case.
Procedural HearingFurther procedural hearings allow the commercial judge to control progress, evidence, legal issues, settlement activity, proof preparation and case timetable.
Case ManagementThe commercial procedure is designed to allow early judicial management, flexible orders, focused evidence and expedited resolution of business disputes.
Expenses and ADRExpenses exposure, settlement offers, mediation and other ADR methods should be assessed early because they can materially affect litigation strategy and commercial leverage.

Appeals

Scottish appeal routes depend on the court of first instance. Commercial actions determined in the Outer House generally proceed to the Inner House. Sheriff Court appeals may proceed through the Sheriff Appeal Court or Inner House according to the applicable route. Further appeal to the UK Supreme Court requires permission.

Outer House AppealAppeals from Outer House commercial decisions generally proceed to the Inner House of the Court of Session.
Sheriff Court AppealAppeals from Sheriff Court decisions follow the applicable Sheriff Appeal Court or Inner House route, depending on the case and statutory framework.
UK Supreme Court ReviewThe UK Supreme Court may grant permission in selected Scottish civil cases raising an arguable point of law of general public importance.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, expenses, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.

Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside merits analysis. The commercial value of a Scottish decree depends on asset availability, enforceability of the result and the practical route to payment, diligence, arrestment, inhibition, attachment or other recovery process.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, company structure, security, banking exposure, timing and likely diligence routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Scottish DiligenceEnforcement may involve charge for payment, arrestment, attachment, inhibition, earnings arrestment where relevant, adjudication, bankruptcy or other diligence mechanisms under Scots law.
Cross-Border DimensionForeign judgment recognition, asset location, service, treaty position, common-law recognition and applicable statutory rules can matter where the dispute extends beyond Scotland.
Cross-Border Relevance

Scotland is an important commercial jurisdiction for energy, renewables, financial services, insurance, professional services, construction, engineering, food and drink, technology, trade and international business. Its separate legal system, Court of Session Commercial Court and Scottish remedies require specific analysis in cross-border disputes.

Scottish Jurisdiction ClausesScottish court clauses and Scots-law governing-law provisions should be assessed independently from England and Wales clauses because Scotland is a separate legal system with separate procedure and remedies.
Commercial Court InterfaceInternational banking, insurance, energy, construction, supply, shipping, trade and complex contract disputes may be appropriate for Court of Session commercial-action procedure.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the correct Scottish court, service pathway, evidence, interim protection, expenses exposure and enforcement destination early.
Arbitration InterfaceArbitration may be an important contractual route in Scottish and international commercial disputes. Its relationship to Court of Session procedure and interim support should be assessed from the outset.
Post-Brexit ConsiderationsJurisdiction, service, recognition and enforcement analysis for EU-connected disputes should be reviewed under the current post-Brexit legal framework rather than assumed from historic EU rules.
Operating Constraints & Risks
Forum RiskCourt of Session, Commercial Court, Sheriff Court and arbitration routes have different jurisdictional, expenses, procedural and appeal implications.
Separate-System RiskScottish procedure, terminology, remedies and court structure differ from England and Wales. An English-law or English-court assumption can produce material forum and strategy errors.
Expenses RiskExpenses exposure, security for expenses and settlement offers can materially affect commercial strategy and settlement leverage.
Interim-Relief RiskDelay may reduce the practical availability or value of interdict, arrestment, inhibition and other urgent protective relief.
Enforcement RiskA favourable decision may have limited commercial value where assets, company structure, international enforcement or diligence have not been assessed.

FAQ

Sheriff Courts may also hear commercial actions, particularly through specialist commercial procedures where available.
What Is the Commercial Court of the Court of Session?The Commercial Court is the specialist commercial-action procedure in the Outer House of the Court of Session. It allows nominated commercial judges to handle a broad range of business disputes quickly and flexibly under Chapter 47 of the Rules of the Court of Session.
What Cases Qualify as Commercial Actions in Scotland?The definition is broad: commercial actions concern transactions or disputes of a commercial or business nature, whether contractual or not. Examples include banking, insurance, sale or supply of goods and services, commercial leases, building contracts, partnership disputes, professional negligence and business property.
