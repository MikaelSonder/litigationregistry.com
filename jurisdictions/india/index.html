Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in India is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from trade, banking, finance, contracts, infrastructure, shareholder relationships, intellectual property, franchising, distribution, joint ventures and other commercial activity. Civil procedure is governed principally by the Code of Civil Procedure 1908, while qualifying commercial disputes are subject to the Commercial Courts Act 2015 and the CPC amendments introduced by that Act.

The Commercial Courts Act establishes Commercial Courts at district level, Commercial Divisions in High Courts with ordinary original civil jurisdiction and Commercial Appellate Divisions or Commercial Appellate Courts for appeals. The regime applies to commercial disputes of a specified value, currently not less than INR 300,000 under the statutory framework. The court system and applicable procedural rules can vary materially by State and High Court.

For businesses, a commercially useful India strategy requires early analysis of the correct State, court and pecuniary jurisdiction; the statutory commercial-dispute definition; pre-institution mediation; strict filing and disclosure expectations; interim relief; appellate route; and the practical ability to enforce against assets.

Court Structure

India has a federal judicial structure in which State-level and High Court arrangements materially affect the correct commercial litigation route. The Commercial Courts Act overlays specialised commercial forums on this wider system for qualifying disputes.

Commercial CourtsCommercial Courts may be constituted at district level after consultation between the State Government and the relevant High Court. They hear commercial disputes of specified value within their territorial jurisdiction.
Commercial DivisionsHigh Courts exercising ordinary original civil jurisdiction have Commercial Divisions to hear qualifying commercial disputes falling within that High Court's jurisdiction.
Commercial Appellate CourtsHear appeals from Commercial Courts at district level where the statutory structure provides for this appellate route.
Commercial Appellate DivisionsHigh Court appellate divisions hear appeals from Commercial Divisions and, in applicable structures, specified commercial appellate matters.
High CourtsEach State or group of States has a High Court. Certain High Courts exercise original civil jurisdiction in addition to appellate jurisdiction.
Supreme Court of IndiaIndia's apex court and final court of appeal, with jurisdiction over qualifying appeals from High Courts and other matters assigned by the Constitution and law.
Procedure

Commercial disputes are governed by the Code of Civil Procedure 1908 as amended by the Commercial Courts Act 2015, together with the Commercial Courts Act and applicable High Court rules. The commercial regime is designed to promote faster, more structured resolution of qualifying disputes, including stricter timelines and case-management provisions.

Commercial DisputeThe statutory definition covers a wide range of business matters, including ordinary merchant, banker, financier and trader transactions, mercantile documents, partnership, joint venture, shareholder, franchise, distribution, management, consultancy, licensing, technology and intellectual-property-related disputes.
Specified ValueA dispute must meet the statutory specified-value threshold. The current minimum specified value under the framework is INR 300,000, subject to applicable rules and notifications.
Pre-Institution MediationA plaintiff generally must exhaust pre-institution mediation before instituting a commercial suit unless urgent interim relief is sought, subject to the applicable legal framework.
CommencementProceedings commence through filing in the competent Commercial Court, Commercial Division or other designated forum with the required pleadings and documents.
Written Case ManagementCommercial procedure includes structured pleadings, disclosure, inspection, admissions, case-management hearings and tighter procedural timelines than ordinary civil litigation.
LanguageProceedings in the Supreme Court and High Courts are in English under the constitutional framework. The language position in subordinate courts may depend on the applicable State and court rules.
Process Flow
1. Dispute AssessmentIdentify the commercial conflict, legal position, business objective, claim value and likely counterparty response.
2. Commercial Route AnalysisDetermine whether the matter is a commercial dispute of specified value and identify the competent State, Commercial Court, Commercial Division or contractual dispute forum.
3. Pre-Institution MediationAssess whether pre-institution mediation is mandatory or whether the claim requires urgent interim relief that supports direct court commencement.
4. Evidence and Filing ReviewOrganise contracts, invoices, correspondence, corporate records, technical material, financial support and disclosure materials.
5. ProceedingsManage pleadings, disclosure, inspection, case management, interim applications, evidence, mediation possibilities and hearings.
6. Judgment and AppealAssess the result, Commercial Appellate Court or Commercial Appellate Division route and whether further Supreme Court review is viable.
7. EnforcementTranslate the result into payment, asset recovery, compliance or settlement leverage.
Appeals

The appellate route depends on the originating commercial forum. Appeals from Commercial Courts may proceed to Commercial Appellate Courts or the relevant High Court structure, while appeals from Commercial Divisions proceed to Commercial Appellate Divisions. Further appeal to the Supreme Court depends on the applicable constitutional and statutory route.

Commercial Court AppealsAppeals from designated Commercial Courts proceed through the statutory commercial appellate route applicable in the relevant State or High Court system.
Commercial Division AppealsAppeals from Commercial Division judgments and orders proceed to the Commercial Appellate Division of the relevant High Court where applicable.
Supreme Court ReviewThe Supreme Court may hear qualifying appeals from High Courts and other courts or tribunals in accordance with its constitutional and statutory jurisdiction.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.
Evidence & Remedies
Documentary EvidenceContracts, amendments, purchase orders, invoices, delivery records, notices, correspondence, corporate materials, payment records and electronic communications are commonly central.
Disclosure and InspectionCommercial Courts Act procedure includes structured disclosure, discovery, inspection, admission and denial processes that should be planned from the outset.
Witness and Expert EvidenceWitness, technical, accounting, valuation or industry evidence may be needed depending on the dispute and the issues in proof.
Interim ProtectionUrgent interim relief may be decisive for asset protection, contractual rights, evidence preservation, injunctions or recovery strategy; it can also affect the pre-institution mediation route.
Primary ReliefCommercial claims may seek payment, performance, damages, declaratory relief, injunctions, protective measures or other available remedies.
Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside merits analysis. The commercial value of an Indian judgment depends on asset availability, enforceability of the result and the practical route to payment or compliance in the relevant State and beyond.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, company records, security, timing and likely execution routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Interim StrategyEarly interim protection may be relevant where there is a material risk of asset dissipation, dissipation of evidence or ineffective enforcement.
Cross-Border DimensionForeign judgment recognition, asset location, reciprocal territory status, international service and applicable treaties or domestic rules can matter where the dispute extends beyond India.
Cross-Border Relevance

India is a major international commercial jurisdiction with a federal court structure and significant cross-border trade, investment, technology and infrastructure activity. International commercial disputes require careful coordination of forum, governing law, service, evidence, interim relief and enforcement.

Forum SelectionCross-border contracts should assess Indian court clauses, arbitration clauses, governing law, territorial jurisdiction, service arrangements, asset location and enforcement destination together.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the proper State and commercial forum, local procedural rules, authority documentation, evidence, translation needs, interim protection and enforcement destination early.
Commercial Courts InterfaceWhere a claim meets the commercial-dispute and specified-value requirements, the Commercial Courts Act route may govern the relevant domestic litigation process.
Practical RiskA valid claim can still lose commercial value if the wrong State or court is selected, statutory mediation requirements are overlooked, documents are incomplete or enforcement has not been planned.
Operating Constraints & Risks
Forum RiskIndia's State, High Court, territorial and pecuniary jurisdiction structure can materially affect the correct commercial litigation route.
Commercial-Qualification RiskThe Commercial Courts Act route depends on the dispute meeting both the commercial-dispute definition and specified-value requirements.
Pre-Mediation RiskFailure to assess pre-institution mediation can delay or complicate commencement where urgent interim relief is not sought.
Evidence and Timeline RiskCommercial procedure imposes structured pleadings, disclosure and timeline requirements; weak early preparation can materially reduce leverage.
Recovery RiskA favourable decision may have limited commercial value where assets are unavailable or enforcement has not been planned.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in India?Commercial disputes of the specified value are heard by designated Commercial Courts at district level, Commercial Divisions of High Courts with ordinary original civil jurisdiction, or other courts designated under the Commercial Courts Act 2015.
What Is the Specified Value for Commercial Disputes in India?Under the Commercial Courts Act, the specified value is not less than INR 300,000, subject to the statutory framework and applicable notification or jurisdictional rules.
Is Pre-Institution Mediation Required for an Indian Commercial Suit?A plaintiff generally must exhaust pre-institution mediation before filing a commercial suit unless the claim contemplates urgent interim relief, subject to the applicable statutory and judicial framework.
What Is the Main Procedural Framework?Commercial litigation is governed by the Code of Civil Procedure 1908 as amended by the Commercial Courts Act 2015, together with the Commercial Courts Act and applicable High Court or court-specific rules.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in India?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in India, but should assess the correct State, forum, jurisdiction clause, evidence, authority documents, pre-institution mediation, interim protection and enforcement planning from the outset.
