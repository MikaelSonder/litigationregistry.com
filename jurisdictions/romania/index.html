Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Romania is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contractual, corporate, payment, insolvency-adjacent or other professional relationships. The main procedural framework is the Romanian Code of Civil Procedure, which establishes rules on jurisdiction, the hearing of civil cases and enforcement of judgments and other enforceable titles.

Romania’s ordinary court system comprises district courts, tribunals, courts of appeal and the High Court of Cassation and Justice. Commercial disputes generally proceed within this civil court architecture rather than through a separate nationwide commercial court system. Court competence can depend on the nature of the claim, its value and territorial factors.

In practical terms, a commercially useful Romanian litigation strategy requires early work on forum, claim framing, evidence, timing, interim protection and recoverability. The best outcome is not only a favourable judgment, but a result that can be converted into payment, compliance, settlement leverage or enforceable recovery.

Court Structure

Romania’s judicial system includes the High Court of Cassation and Justice and the other courts of law. The ordinary hierarchy includes district courts, tribunals, courts of appeal and the High Court of Cassation and Justice.

District CourtsFirst-instance courts for matters assigned to them under the procedural rules.
TribunalsFirst-instance or appellate role depending on the matter; relevant for certain civil and professional disputes and for appeals from district courts.
Courts of AppealHear appeals in the ordinary hierarchy and operate through specialised sections or panels, including for company, trade-register, insolvency and unfair-competition matters where applicable.
High Court of Cassation and JusticeHighest court in the ordinary judicial system; ordinarily relevant at the final review stage rather than as a normal first-instance commercial court.
SpecialisationCommercial matters are generally dealt with inside the civil court structure. Romania has limited specialised civil courts and specialised sections or panels for certain categories of disputes.
Procedure

The Romanian Code of Civil Procedure is the central procedural framework. It regulates competence, the hearing of civil cases and enforcement of judgments and other enforceable titles.

Forum and CompetenceThe correct court depends on subject matter, claim value and territorial connection. Forum analysis should be completed before filing.
CommencementCivil proceedings commence through submission of the claim to the competent court, subject to any prior procedure required by law.
Pleadings and Case FramingThe claim and defence should set out the factual basis, legal grounds, relief sought and supporting documents with procedural discipline.
Case ManagementProceedings are developed through service, submissions, evidential steps, hearings and judicial directions within the applicable procedural framework.
Commercial CharacterRomanian law commonly addresses disputes between professionals within the civil-law framework rather than through a separate general commercial-law court system.

1. Dispute AssessmentIdentify the commercial conflict, legal position, business objective and likely counterparty response.
2. Forum AnalysisDetermine the competent Romanian court by subject matter, value, territory and any cross-border element.
3. Evidence ReviewOrganise contracts, invoices, correspondence, corporate records, technical material and financial support.

6. Judgment and AppealAssess the result, applicable appeal route and whether further review is commercially justified.
7. EnforcementTranslate the result into payment, compliance, asset recovery or settlement leverage.
Appeals

The route of appeal depends on the court that heard the matter at first instance and the nature of the decision. In the ordinary hierarchy, matters originating before district courts may move to tribunals and then, where available, to courts of appeal; matters originating before tribunals may move to courts of appeal and, in relevant cases, to the High Court of Cassation and Justice.

First-Instance ReviewAppeal options are determined by the applicable procedural rules and the court of first instance.
Higher ReviewCourts of appeal and the High Court of Cassation and Justice may be relevant depending on the case route and available remedy.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed not only on legal grounds, but also on timing, cost, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.
Evidence & Remedies
Documentary EvidenceContracts, amendments, orders, invoices, delivery records, notices, correspondence, board materials and payment records are typically central to commercial disputes.
Witness and Expert EvidenceWitness, technical or financial evidence may be needed depending on the nature of the dispute and the issues in proof.
Interim ProtectionUrgent procedural measures may be relevant where delay risks assets, evidence, contractual rights or effective recovery.
Primary ReliefCommercial claims may seek payment, performance, damages, declaratory relief, protective measures or other remedies available under the applicable substantive and procedural rules.
Enforcement

The Code of Civil Procedure also governs enforcement of court judgments and other enforceable titles. Enforcement planning should begin before judgment where possible, particularly when recoverability, asset location or cross-border elements affect the commercial value of the claim.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, security, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed alongside merits.
Cross-Border DimensionEU rules and applicable international instruments can matter where the judgment, debtor, assets or relevant business activity extends beyond Romania.
Cross-Border Relevance

Romania is an EU Member State. Cross-border commercial litigation may therefore require analysis of EU rules on jurisdiction, service, evidence and recognition or enforcement, alongside Romanian court competence and domestic procedure.

Forum SelectionCross-border cases should assess contractual jurisdiction clauses, the defendant’s establishment, place of performance, applicable EU rules and domestic competence rules.
European Union InterfaceEU judicial-cooperation rules can be central to civil and commercial disputes involving parties, evidence, assets or judgments in different Member States.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the proper forum, service pathway, language and document requirements, potential interim protection and enforcement destination early.
Practical RiskA valid claim can still lose commercial value if it is filed in the wrong forum, poorly documented or unsupported by an enforcement plan.

Recovery RiskA favourable decision may have limited commercial value where assets are unavailable or enforcement has not been planned.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in Romania?Commercial disputes are generally handled within the ordinary civil court system. Competence depends on the claim, its value, subject matter and territorial connection; first-instance matters may be heard by district courts or tribunals.
Does Romania Have Separate Commercial Courts?Romania does not operate a separate nationwide commercial court system. Commercial disputes are generally treated as disputes between professionals within the civil court structure, while certain specialised courts or panels may deal with relevant matters.
What Is the Main Procedural Framework?The Romanian Code of Civil Procedure establishes rules on jurisdiction, the hearing of civil cases and enforcement of judgments and other enforceable titles.
What Is the Appeal Structure?The ordinary hierarchy includes district courts, tribunals, courts of appeal and the High Court of Cassation and Justice. The appeal route depends on the court and matter at first instance.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Romania?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Romania, but should assess forum, service, evidence, document formalities, language, timing and enforcement planning from the outset.
