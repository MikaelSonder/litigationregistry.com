Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Saudi Arabia is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from trade, commercial contracts, banking, finance, company relationships, bankruptcy, intellectual property, agency, distribution, construction and other commercial activity. The principal specialist framework is the Law of Commercial Courts, approved by Royal Decree No. M/93 in 2020, together with its Implementing Regulations.

Commercial Courts are specialised first-instance courts within the general judiciary. Their jurisdiction includes disputes between merchants connected with their business, specified commercial-contract claims against merchants, company-law, bankruptcy-law, intellectual-property-law and other commercial-law claims, as well as related requests concerning judicial receivers, trustees, liquidators and experts. Appeals proceed through commercial circuits in the Courts of Appeal, with qualifying legal review by the commercial circuit of the Supreme Court.

For businesses, a commercially useful Saudi strategy requires early work on commercial-court competence, mandatory pre-litigation requirements where applicable, Arabic-language documentation, electronic filings, interim protection, expert evidence, appeal exposure and enforcement. The Saudi commercial judiciary has a strong electronic-service component through Ministry of Justice systems and the Najiz platform.

Court Structure

Saudi Arabia's commercial judiciary operates through specialised Commercial Courts within the general court system. The broader judicial hierarchy includes first-instance courts, Courts of Appeal and the Supreme Court, with commercial circuits handling qualifying commercial matters at the appellate and Supreme Court levels.

Commercial CourtsSpecialised first-instance courts that hear commercial disputes within the jurisdiction set by the Law of Commercial Courts and its Implementing Regulations.
Commercial Circuits of Courts of AppealReview appeals from Commercial Court judgments and decisions according to the appellate procedure and jurisdiction rules.
Commercial Circuit of Supreme CourtReviews qualifying appeals from appellate commercial circuits on grounds including violation, misapplication or misinterpretation of Sharia or law, procedural breach or conflict with Supreme Court judicial principles.
General CourtsIn locations without a specialised Commercial Court, commercial circuits in general courts may exercise the relevant commercial jurisdiction under the applicable judicial arrangements.
Enforcement CourtsEnforcement functions are handled through the relevant execution framework and should be treated as a separate operational phase after an enforceable title is obtained.
Electronic ServiceCommercial Courts provide electronic case and procedural services through Ministry of Justice systems, including the Najiz platform.
Procedure

The Law of Commercial Courts and its Implementing Regulations establish specialist jurisdiction and procedure. They regulate commercial-court filing, hearings, evidence, electronic litigation, preliminary requirements, case management and appeals. Arabic is the operative court language, requiring early translation and formal-document planning for foreign parties.

Forum and CompetenceCommercial Court competence should be assessed by the commercial nature of the dispute, trader status, claim type, claim value where relevant, party relationship and territorial connection.
Commercial JurisdictionThe Commercial Courts hear merchant disputes connected with business, defined commercial-contract claims against merchants, company, bankruptcy, IP and other commercial-law claims, and related requests for receivers, trustees, liquidators and experts.
CommencementProceedings are commenced through the competent Commercial Court or commercial circuit using the prescribed electronic and procedural route.
Electronic LitigationElectronic litigation procedures are carried out through Ministry of Justice approved systems. Filing, memorandum exchange, remote litigation, objections and case-date inquiries may be available through Najiz services.
Pre-Litigation RequirementsPrior notices, conciliation, mediation or other preliminary steps may be relevant depending on the type of dispute and current regulations. The precise route should be checked before filing.
Language and FormalitiesArabic-language pleadings and evidence are central. Foreign documents require early planning for certified translation, authentication, legalisation or apostille where applicable.

Appeals

Commercial Court judgments are generally subject to appeal before commercial circuits of the Courts of Appeal. The commercial circuit of the Supreme Court reviews qualifying appellate decisions on legal and procedural grounds rather than as a new factual trial.

Commercial AppealAppeals from Commercial Court judgments and decisions are heard by the competent commercial circuit of the Court of Appeal under the applicable law and procedure.
Supreme Court ReviewThe Supreme Court commercial circuit may review appellate commercial decisions for violation, misapplication or misinterpretation of Sharia or law, procedural breach and other statutory review grounds.
Legal CharacterSupreme Court review focuses on legal and procedural correctness rather than a full new assessment of facts and evidence.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.

Interim ProtectionUrgent measures may be relevant where delay risks assets, evidence, contractual rights or effective recovery. Available relief and requirements should be assessed under the applicable commercial and execution framework.
Primary ReliefCommercial claims may seek payment, performance, damages, declarations, protective measures or other relief available under Sharia, law and applicable commercial procedure.
Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside merits analysis. The commercial value of a Saudi judgment depends on asset availability, enforceability of the result and the practical route to payment or compliance through the Kingdom's execution framework.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, company records, security, bank exposure, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Electronic InterfaceMinistry of Justice electronic systems and Najiz services may support procedural interaction, but execution strategy should be assessed separately from the merits case.
Cross-Border DimensionForeign judgment recognition, arbitral award enforcement, asset location, international service and applicable treaty or reciprocity rules can matter where the dispute extends beyond Saudi Arabia.
Cross-Border Relevance

Saudi Arabia is a major regional commercial jurisdiction with significant international investment, procurement, trade, energy, infrastructure and technology activity. Cross-border disputes require early attention to Saudi Commercial Court competence, Arabic procedure, contractual forum clauses, arbitration agreements, asset location and enforceability.

Forum SelectionCross-border contracts should assess Saudi Commercial Court clauses, arbitration clauses, governing law, service arrangements, asset location and enforcement destination together.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the correct commercial forum, Arabic-language procedure, authority documents, evidence, translations, potential interim protection and enforcement destination early.
Electronic LitigationElectronic filing and litigation functions can support procedural management, but foreign parties should ensure reliable local access, authority and document handling.
Arbitration InterfaceArbitration may be an important alternative or parallel contractual route in international business disputes. Its relationship to Saudi court jurisdiction and enforcement should be assessed from the outset.
Practical RiskA jurisdiction or arbitration clause is commercially useful only when it aligns with the parties, contract, procedural language, evidence, assets and realistic enforcement route.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in Saudi Arabia?Commercial Courts are specialised first-instance courts within Saudi Arabia’s general judiciary. Appeals are heard by commercial circuits of the Courts of Appeal, while a commercial circuit of the Supreme Court reviews qualifying appellate decisions.
What Is the Main Law Governing Saudi Commercial Litigation?The Law of Commercial Courts, approved by Royal Decree No. M/93 in 2020, and its Implementing Regulations provide the central specialist framework for commercial court jurisdiction and procedure.
What Disputes Fall Within the Saudi Commercial Courts’ Jurisdiction?Commercial Courts hear disputes between merchants connected with their business, specified claims against merchants under commercial contracts, company, bankruptcy, intellectual property and other commercial-law claims, as well as related receivership, liquidation and expert appointments.
Can Commercial Litigation Be Conducted Electronically in Saudi Arabia?Yes. The Implementing Regulations provide that electronic litigation procedures are carried out through Ministry of Justice approved electronic systems. Commercial Courts also provide electronic services through the Najiz portal.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Saudi Arabia?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Saudi Arabia, but should assess Commercial Court competence, Arabic-language procedure, authority documents, evidence, translations, electronic access, interim protection and enforcement planning from the outset.
