Commercial disputes in the UAE require early forum analysis because the country combines federal courts, emirate-level court systems and English-language common-law courts in DIFC and ADGM financial free zones.

Effective planning depends on the emirate, contract, jurisdiction clause, governing law, court language, party structure, asset location, interim protection and the practical route from judgment to enforcement.

Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in the United Arab Emirates is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, trade, banking, finance, construction, real estate, corporate relationships, commercial papers, insolvency and other business activity. The UAE has a multi-layered judicial environment rather than a single uniform commercial court system.

Onshore litigation may proceed through the federal judiciary or through local emirate court systems. Abu Dhabi, Dubai and Ras Al Khaimah maintain independent local judicial systems, while federal courts operate in other emirates subject to the allocation of jurisdiction. Onshore civil and commercial procedure is governed principally by Federal Decree-Law No. 42 of 2022 promulgating the Civil Procedure Code.

The UAE also contains two English-language common-law financial-free-zone court systems: DIFC Courts in Dubai and ADGM Courts in Abu Dhabi. These can provide a separate route for qualifying civil and commercial disputes, including disputes submitted by written party agreement. For businesses, the decisive issue is usually forum architecture: selecting the correct onshore, local, DIFC or ADGM route before the dispute escalates.

Court Structure

The UAE has a dual judicial system comprising federal courts and local emirate courts, alongside common-law courts in the DIFC and ADGM financial free zones. Correct court selection is a threshold commercial issue.

Federal CourtsFederal courts hear matters assigned to the federal judiciary, including civil and commercial matters in emirates operating under the federal system. The federal hierarchy includes Courts of First Instance, Courts of Appeal and the Federal Supreme Court.
Local Emirate CourtsAbu Dhabi, Dubai and Ras Al Khaimah maintain independent local judicial systems, generally with Courts of First Instance, Courts of Appeal and Courts of Cassation. Sharjah has also developed an independent local judicial system.
Commercial CircuitsOnshore courts use civil and commercial circuits or chambers. The relevant circuit can depend on the commercial nature of the dispute, party status and claim value.
DIFC CourtsIndependent English-language common-law courts in Dubai International Financial Centre. Their civil and commercial jurisdiction may arise from DIFC connection or written party agreement, subject to applicable rules.
ADGM CourtsEnglish-language common-law courts in Abu Dhabi Global Market. Their jurisdiction may arise from ADGM connection or written party agreement, subject to applicable rules.
Appeal StructureOnshore cases proceed through federal or local appellate routes. DIFC and ADGM Courts have their own court hierarchies and appellate arrangements.
Procedure

Federal Decree-Law No. 42 of 2022 promulgating the Civil Procedure Code regulates civil and commercial litigation procedure, jurisdiction, appeals and execution before UAE onshore courts. DIFC Courts and ADGM Courts apply their own common-law procedural regimes. The procedural model selected can materially affect language, pleadings, evidence, hearings, costs and enforcement planning.

Forum and CompetenceIn commercial matters, jurisdiction may lie where the defendant is domiciled, where the agreement was made or performed wholly or partly, or where it should have been performed, subject to the Civil Procedure Code and other applicable rules.
Onshore CommencementOnshore proceedings are commenced through the competent federal or local court route, with procedural requirements determined by the Civil Procedure Code and relevant court practice.
LanguageArabic is the official language of onshore UAE courts. The Civil Procedure Code permits English in designated specialist tribunals or proceedings where the competent judicial authority decides accordingly. DIFC and ADGM Courts operate in English.
Financial Free Zone RouteDIFC and ADGM court jurisdiction should be assessed through the free-zone connection, statutory scope and any written jurisdiction agreement between the parties.
Pleadings and EvidenceThe claim and defence should establish the factual basis, legal grounds, relief sought and supporting evidence in a form appropriate to the selected court system and language.
Interim ProtectionUrgent measures may be relevant where delay risks assets, evidence, contractual rights or effective recovery. The available tools and standards depend on the forum selected.

Appeals

Appeal routes depend on the court system selected. Onshore federal and local court systems generally operate three tiers: Court of First Instance, Court of Appeal and a final federal or local cassation-level court. DIFC and ADGM Courts operate their own appellate structures.

Federal RouteFederal cases generally move from the Court of First Instance to the Court of Appeal and, where permitted, to the Federal Supreme Court.
Local Emirate RouteDubai, Abu Dhabi and Ras Al Khaimah local court systems generally operate through Courts of First Instance, Courts of Appeal and Courts of Cassation.
DIFC and ADGM RouteDIFC Courts and ADGM Courts have their own appellate structures under their respective laws and court rules.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.
Evidence & Remedies
Documentary EvidenceContracts, amendments, orders, invoices, delivery records, notices, correspondence, corporate materials, banking records, commercial papers and digital communications are commonly central.
Language and TranslationOnshore proceedings require careful Arabic-language planning. English documents may require translation unless a designated English-language procedure applies. DIFC and ADGM proceedings operate in English.
Witness and Expert EvidenceWitness, technical, accounting, valuation, financial or foreign-law evidence may be needed depending on the dispute and the selected court system.
Interim ProtectionAttachment, preservation, injunction-type and other urgent protective measures may be relevant where delay risks assets, evidence, contractual rights or effective recovery.
Primary ReliefCommercial claims may seek payment, performance, damages, declarations, protective measures or other remedies available in the selected forum.
Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside merits analysis. In the UAE, the commercial value of a judgment depends not only on asset availability, but also on the relationship between the originating court, the intended enforcement forum and the relevant UAE or foreign enforcement route.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, company structure, security, bank exposure, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Inter-System InterfaceEnforcement between federal or local onshore courts and DIFC or ADGM Courts requires careful analysis of the applicable statutory, procedural and memorandum-based mechanisms.
Cross-Border DimensionForeign judgment recognition, asset location, international service, treaty position and reciprocity can matter where the dispute extends beyond the UAE.
Cross-Border Relevance

The UAE is a major regional business, finance, trade and logistics hub. Its multi-forum court architecture, DIFC and ADGM common-law courts, Arabic and English language options, and cross-border asset structures make forum selection particularly important in international commercial disputes.

Forum SelectionCross-border contracts should assess onshore UAE, Dubai, Abu Dhabi, DIFC, ADGM and arbitration clauses together with governing law, service arrangements, asset location and enforcement destination.
DIFC and ADGM OptionsDIFC and ADGM Courts may be chosen by written party agreement for qualifying civil and commercial disputes, subject to their respective jurisdictional rules.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the proper UAE forum, court language, service pathway, authority documentation, evidence, interim protection and enforcement destination early.
Onshore English OptionArabic remains the official onshore court language, but English may be authorised for designated specialist tribunals or proceedings under the Civil Procedure Code.
Practical RiskA jurisdiction clause is commercially useful only when it aligns with the parties, contract, chosen law, procedural language, assets and realistic enforcement route.
Operating Constraints & Risks
Forum RiskFederal, local emirate, DIFC and ADGM court routes have different jurisdictional foundations, legal traditions, languages, procedures and appeal structures.
Language RiskArabic procedure, English financial-free-zone procedure and translation requirements must be planned before filing and evidence preparation.
Jurisdiction-Clause RiskForum clauses must be assessed carefully for validity, scope, selected legal system and enforceability in the relevant court architecture.
Evidence and Formality RiskWeak document control, inadequate authority evidence or missing translation and formalisation can materially reduce litigation leverage.
Recovery RiskA favourable decision may have limited commercial value where assets are unavailable or inter-system and cross-border enforcement has not been planned.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in the UAE?Commercial disputes may proceed through federal courts, local emirate court systems, or financial-free-zone common-law courts such as DIFC Courts and ADGM Courts. The correct forum depends on the emirate, parties, contract, subject matter, jurisdiction clause and applicable law.
What Is the Main Civil Procedure Law in the UAE?Federal Decree-Law No. 42 of 2022 promulgating the Civil Procedure Code regulates civil and commercial litigation procedure, jurisdiction, appeals and execution before UAE courts.
Can Commercial Proceedings Be Conducted in English in UAE Onshore Courts?Arabic is the official court language. However, the Civil Procedure Code permits designated specialised tribunals or proceedings to use English where the competent judicial authority makes the required decision.
What Are DIFC and ADGM Courts?DIFC Courts and ADGM Courts are English-language common-law courts operating in financial free zones in Dubai and Abu Dhabi. Their jurisdiction can arise from the free-zone connection or the parties’ written agreement, subject to the applicable court rules.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in the UAE?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in the UAE, but should assess the correct forum, court language, jurisdiction clause, authority documents, evidence, translations, interim protection and enforcement planning from the outset.
