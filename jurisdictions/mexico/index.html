Commercial disputes in Mexico are governed principally by the Código de Comercio and may proceed through federal or local courts with commercial jurisdiction. Correct court, procedure and remedy selection are central from the outset.

Mexico's oral commercial procedure is a defining feature of the system. It is designed around oral hearings, immediacy, continuity and concentrated case handling, while executive and ordinary commercial routes remain relevant for other claim types.

Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Mexico is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from acts of commerce, contracts, debt, negotiable instruments, company relationships, distribution, finance, trade and other commercial activity. The central procedural framework is the Código de Comercio, whose Book Five governs commercial proceedings.

Mexico is a federal state. Commercial disputes may be heard by federal or local courts with commercial competence, depending on the nature of the claim, the jurisdictional basis, the parties, the territorial connection and the applicable rules. The procedural route may be ordinary commercial, oral commercial, executive commercial, oral executive commercial or a special proceeding regulated by commercial law.

A defining feature is the Juicio Oral Mercantil, the oral commercial procedure. It is designed around principles of orality, publicity, equality, immediacy, adversarial process, continuity and concentration. For businesses, a commercially useful Mexico strategy requires early work on forum, procedure selection, documentary evidence, enforceable instruments, interim protection, constitutional review exposure and actual recoverability.

Court Structure

Mexico's commercial litigation environment is shaped by the federal structure. Commercial jurisdiction may be exercised by federal courts or by local courts applying federal commercial law. The actual court and appeal route varies by State, judicial district, amount, subject matter and procedural path.

Federal CourtsFederal courts hear commercial matters assigned to federal jurisdiction and may become relevant where the claim, parties, instrument, constitutional issue or other statutory basis creates federal competence.
Local CourtsState courts frequently hear commercial disputes under concurrent jurisdiction arrangements and apply the Código de Comercio and applicable procedural rules.
Commercial Judges or CourtsMany federal and local judicial systems designate commercial judges, commercial chambers or commercial oral-proceeding courts for mercantile matters.
Oral Commercial CourtsCourts or specialised hearing structures may manage Juicio Oral Mercantil and Juicio Ejecutivo Mercantil Oral proceedings under the Code of Commerce.
Appellate ReviewThe appeal route depends on the originating federal or local court, the procedure selected and the applicable commercial procedural rules.
Constitutional ReviewAmparo proceedings can be relevant to challenge judicial acts or final decisions on constitutional grounds, subject to the applicable Mexican constitutional and procedural framework.
Procedure

The Código de Comercio distinguishes between ordinary, oral, executive and special commercial proceedings. Commercial litigation procedure is federal in character, but the competent court can be federal or local. Correct selection of the procedure is central because the procedural route affects hearings, evidence, remedies, review and timing.

Commercial ProceedingsCommercial proceedings are those intended to hear and decide disputes derived from acts of commerce. The Código de Comercio provides the principal procedural framework.
Ordinary Commercial ProcedureUsed for commercial disputes that do not follow a specific special or executive route and where the legal conditions for another procedure are not met.
Oral Commercial ProcedureJuicio Oral Mercantil is governed by the special title in Book Five of the Código de Comercio and follows principles of orality, publicity, equality, immediacy, contradiction, continuity and concentration.
Executive Commercial ProcedureJuicio Ejecutivo Mercantil may be relevant where the claimant holds a qualifying enforceable commercial instrument or document and seeks accelerated debt recovery or attachment-related relief.
Oral Executive Commercial ProcedureJuicio Ejecutivo Mercantil Oral provides an oral executive route under the applicable Code of Commerce provisions, particularly relevant to qualifying commercial debt claims.
Supplementary ProcedureWhere the Código de Comercio and special commercial laws do not regulate an issue, the applicable supplementary civil procedural law may apply, subject to the current legal framework.

Appeals & Review

The availability of appeal depends on the commercial procedure used and the originating court. Ordinary appeals can be available in appropriate commercial proceedings, while the Code of Commerce provides that no ordinary appeal lies against decisions issued in Juicio Oral Mercantil. Constitutional amparo review may nevertheless be relevant in qualifying circumstances.

Ordinary AppealThe availability and route of ordinary appeal depend on the claim type, procedure, court of origin and applicable commercial procedural rules.
Oral Commercial FinalityThe Code of Commerce provides that no ordinary appeal lies against decisions rendered in oral commercial proceedings.
Constitutional ReviewAmparo may be relevant for judicial acts or final decisions in qualifying circumstances under the constitutional review framework.
Commercial DecisionReview should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, enforceability impact and settlement leverage.

Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside merits analysis. The commercial value of a Mexican judgment depends on asset availability, the enforceability of the result and the practical route to payment, attachment or compliance in the relevant State or federal system.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, commercial instruments, company records, security, timing and likely attachment or execution routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Executive RouteWhere a qualifying commercial instrument exists, executive commercial procedure may provide a strategically important route to debt recovery and asset-protection measures.
Cross-Border DimensionForeign judgment recognition, arbitral award enforcement, asset location, international service and applicable treaty or domestic rules can matter where the dispute extends beyond Mexico.
Cross-Border Relevance

Mexico is a major North American and Latin American commercial jurisdiction with extensive cross-border trade, manufacturing, investment, logistics and supply-chain activity. International commercial disputes require early coordination of forum, governing law, service, evidence, instruments, interim relief and enforcement.

Forum SelectionCross-border contracts should assess Mexican court clauses, arbitration clauses, governing law, federal or local competence, service arrangements, asset location and enforcement destination together.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the correct Mexican forum, Spanish-language procedure, authority documents, evidence, translation, commercial instruments, interim protection and enforcement destination early.
Oral Commercial RouteCross-border commercial claims should assess whether the oral commercial procedure is available and commercially suitable, taking into account its hearing structure and ordinary-appeal limits.
Arbitration InterfaceArbitration may be an important contractual route in international business disputes. Its relationship to Mexican court jurisdiction, interim relief and enforcement should be assessed from the outset.
Practical RiskA valid claim can still lose commercial value if the wrong federal or local court is selected, the commercial procedure is misidentified, evidence is incomplete or enforcement has not been planned.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in Mexico?Commercial disputes may be heard by federal or local courts with commercial jurisdiction. The correct court depends on federal or local competence, the parties, subject matter, territorial connection and the applicable commercial procedure.
What Law Governs Commercial Litigation in Mexico?The Código de Comercio is the principal federal framework for commercial acts and commercial proceedings. It provides for ordinary, oral, executive and special commercial proceedings, supplemented where applicable by procedural law.
What Is Juicio Oral Mercantil?Juicio Oral Mercantil is Mexico’s oral commercial procedure. It is governed by the Code of Commerce and follows principles including orality, publicity, equality, immediacy, adversarial process, continuity and concentration.
Are Appeals Available in Oral Commercial Proceedings?The Code of Commerce provides that no ordinary appeal lies against decisions issued in the oral commercial procedure, although extraordinary constitutional or other remedies may be relevant depending on the case.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Mexico?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Mexico, but should assess the correct federal or local forum, Spanish-language procedure, authority documents, commercial instruments, evidence, interim protection and enforcement planning from the outset.
