Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in China is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, payment obligations, corporate relationships, supply chains, trade, finance, technology, construction and other professional transactions. The central procedural framework is the Civil Procedure Law of the People's Republic of China.

China’s judicial system is organised through the people's courts. Basic and intermediate people's courts generally handle commercial disputes at first instance according to statutory competence, higher people's courts hear appeals, and the Supreme People's Court is the highest judicial organ. Commercial matters are generally handled within this court structure rather than through a separate nationwide commercial court system.

China has also created the China International Commercial Court (CICC), a permanent adjudicatory organ of the Supreme People's Court for qualifying international commercial cases. For businesses, a commercially useful China strategy requires early work on court competence, Chinese-language evidence, legal representation, property preservation, service, appeal exposure, asset location and actual enforceability.

Court Structure

China's people's court system operates through multiple levels. The relevant first-instance court depends on the amount in dispute, subject matter, territorial connection, foreign-related elements and statutory allocation of competence.

Basic People's CourtsHandle many first-instance civil and commercial matters within their statutory competence.
Intermediate People's CourtsHear first-instance cases assigned by law, including defined foreign-related, high-value or specialised matters, and hear appeals from basic people's courts.
Higher People's CourtsHear appeals from intermediate people's courts and first-instance matters assigned by law or court competence rules.
Supreme People's CourtChina's highest judicial organ. It hears cases assigned to it by law, reviews defined matters and issues judicial interpretations guiding court practice.
China International Commercial CourtA permanent adjudicatory organ of the Supreme People's Court established to adjudicate qualifying international commercial cases. The First International Commercial Court is in Shenzhen and the Second is in Xi'an.
Specialised Courts and TribunalsDefined subject areas, including certain intellectual property, maritime, financial or internet-related matters, may have specialised jurisdiction routes that should be assessed separately.
Procedure

The Civil Procedure Law governs civil litigation. Significant amendments effective from 1 January 2024 affect foreign-related civil and commercial litigation, including jurisdiction, service, evidence abroad and recognition or enforcement of foreign judgments. Court procedure is conducted in Chinese, and foreign parties should plan early for translations and formal evidence handling.

Forum and CompetenceFor contract disputes, competence can be linked to the defendant's domicile or the place of contract performance. Foreign-related jurisdiction may also connect to contract signature or performance, assets, tort location, representative office or another appropriate connection with China.
CommencementA civil action is commenced by filing a complaint with the people's court having jurisdiction. The complaint should state the parties, claims, facts and legal grounds.
Pleadings and Case FramingThe claim and defence should establish the factual basis, legal grounds, requested relief and supporting evidence in a coherent court-ready form.
LanguageChinese is the operational language of court proceedings. Foreign parties should plan for Chinese translations, document authentication or legalisation where required, and local representation.
ServiceService should be analysed early in foreign-related cases because cross-border service can affect timing, procedural validity and strategic sequencing.
Property PreservationPre-action or in-action property preservation may be commercially important where recovery risk, asset dissipation or urgent protection is present.
Process Flow
1. Dispute AssessmentIdentify the commercial conflict, legal position, business objective and likely counterparty response.
2. Jurisdiction AnalysisDetermine the competent people's court by defendant domicile, contract performance, subject matter, amount, assets, foreign-related links and any forum clause.
3. Evidence and Preservation ReviewOrganise contracts, invoices, correspondence, corporate records, technical material and financial support; assess whether preservation measures are required.
4. Complaint or DefencePrepare the procedural submission, requested relief and legal basis in a coherent Chinese-language court-ready form.
5. ProceedingsManage service, written submissions, evidence, hearings, mediation possibilities, preservation measures and procedural applications where relevant.
6. Judgment and AppealAssess the result, available appeal route and whether further review is legally and commercially justified.
7. EnforcementTranslate the result into payment, asset recovery, compliance or settlement leverage.
Appeals

China generally applies a two-instance final system: a first-instance judgment may be appealed once to the next higher people's court, and the second-instance judgment is generally final. Separate trial-supervision or retrial mechanisms may exist in prescribed circumstances but should not be treated as a routine additional appeal.

First AppealA party dissatisfied with a first-instance judgment may generally appeal to the next higher people's court within the applicable statutory period.
Second InstanceThe second-instance court reviews the appeal and its judgment is generally final under the two-instance final system.
Trial SupervisionRetrial or trial-supervision mechanisms may be available in prescribed circumstances, but they are exceptional review routes rather than a standard third appeal.
CICC DecisionsJudgments of the China International Commercial Court are final and binding because the CICC is part of the Supreme People's Court.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, preservation, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.
Evidence & Remedies
Documentary EvidenceContracts, amendments, orders, invoices, delivery records, notices, correspondence, corporate materials, seals, payment records and digital communications are commonly central.
Foreign EvidenceForeign-language materials require translation. Formalities such as notarisation, authentication or legalisation may be relevant depending on the document and procedural purpose.
Witness and Expert EvidenceWitness, technical, accounting, valuation or industry evidence may be needed depending on the dispute and the issues in proof.
Preservation MeasuresProperty, conduct and evidence preservation can be significant tools where delay risks asset dissipation, evidence loss or ineffective recovery.
Primary ReliefCommercial claims may seek payment, performance, damages, declaratory relief, preservation measures or other available remedies.
Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside merits analysis. The commercial value of a Chinese judgment depends on asset availability, enforceability of the result and the practical route to payment or compliance.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, company registration, security, timing and likely preservation or enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Property PreservationEarly property preservation can be important where there is a risk that assets will be transferred, dissipated or otherwise unavailable by the enforcement stage.
Foreign JudgmentsThe current Civil Procedure Law provides routes for applying to an intermediate people's court for recognition and enforcement of an effective foreign judgment under applicable treaty or reciprocity principles.
Cross-Border DimensionForeign judgment recognition, international service, asset location and applicable treaties or reciprocity can matter where the dispute extends beyond China.
Cross-Border Relevance

China is a major international commercial jurisdiction. The 2024 Civil Procedure Law framework materially affects foreign-related civil and commercial litigation, including jurisdiction, service abroad, evidence abroad and foreign judgment recognition or enforcement. Cross-border cases require early and detailed strategic preparation.

International JurisdictionForeign-related commercial disputes can connect to China through contract signature or performance, subject matter, assets, tort location, a representative office or other appropriate connection, subject to the current Civil Procedure Law framework.
China International Commercial CourtThe CICC may hear qualifying international commercial cases selected by the parties for Supreme People's Court jurisdiction, transferred with approval from higher people's courts, of nationwide significance, or involving specified arbitration-support functions.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the proper people's court, service pathway, Chinese-language and translation requirements, preservation options, authority documentation and enforcement destination early.
Hong Kong DistinctionHong Kong operates as a separate common-law jurisdiction and is covered by a separate Commercial Litigation Registry record. It should not be treated as part of this mainland China court record.
Practical RiskA legally valid claim can still lose commercial value if documentation is not litigation-ready in Chinese, the court is poorly selected or preservation and enforcement have not been planned.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in China?Commercial disputes are generally heard within the people’s court system. Basic and intermediate people’s courts hear cases at first instance depending on competence, higher people’s courts hear appeals and the Supreme People’s Court is the highest judicial organ.
What Is the China International Commercial Court?The China International Commercial Court is a permanent adjudicatory organ of the Supreme People’s Court for qualifying international commercial cases. Its First International Commercial Court is located in Shenzhen and its Second International Commercial Court is located in Xi’an.
What Law Governs Civil and Commercial Litigation in China?The Civil Procedure Law of the People’s Republic of China provides the central procedural framework for civil litigation, including foreign-related civil and commercial cases. Significant amendments took effect on 1 January 2024.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate Commercial Disputes in China?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in China, but should assess jurisdiction, Chinese-language procedure, service, evidence, translation, preservation measures, recognition and enforcement planning at an early stage.
Does This Record Cover Hong Kong?No. Hong Kong has its own legal system and its own dedicated registry page. This record concerns the People’s Republic of China mainland court system.
