Florida commercial litigation frequently involves real estate, finance, trade, hospitality, insurance, corporate disputes, international business and Latin America-facing activity. Correct circuit, venue, complex-division eligibility and federal-court analysis are central from the outset.

Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Florida is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, real estate, hospitality, trade, finance, insurance, corporate relationships, construction, distribution, franchise activity, technology and other commercial transactions. Most substantial Florida state-law commercial disputes are filed in circuit courts.

Florida does not operate a single statewide business court. Instead, several judicial circuits have established specialised Complex Business Litigation or Business Court divisions within their circuit civil courts. These include the Eleventh Judicial Circuit in Miami-Dade County, the Seventeenth Judicial Circuit in Broward County, the Ninth Judicial Circuit in Orange and Osceola Counties, and the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit in Hillsborough County.

For businesses, a commercially useful Florida litigation strategy requires early analysis of circuit and county venue, complex-division eligibility, contract clauses, discovery, interim relief, insurance, appeal route and federal court removal or filing options. The local nature of Florida's specialist business dockets makes county selection particularly important.

Court Structure

Florida's state court system includes county courts, circuit courts, District Courts of Appeal and the Florida Supreme Court. Major commercial claims generally proceed in circuit courts. The availability of specialist business divisions depends on the local judicial circuit rather than a statewide commercial court statute.

Florida Circuit CourtsTrial courts of general jurisdiction for major civil and commercial disputes, including substantial contract, business tort, real estate, corporate and other commercial claims.
County CourtsHear civil matters within their statutory monetary and subject-matter jurisdiction and should be distinguished from circuit court commercial litigation.
Complex Business Litigation DivisionsSpecialised business dockets within selected circuit courts, designed for complex commercial cases requiring focused case management and judicial expertise.
District Courts of AppealIntermediate appellate courts reviewing final circuit court judgments and appealable orders in their respective appellate districts.
Florida Supreme CourtFlorida's highest state court, exercising discretionary review over selected state-law issues, conflict jurisdiction and other matters assigned by the Florida Constitution and law.
Federal District CourtsFederal trial courts in the Northern, Middle and Southern Districts of Florida hear federal-question and qualifying diversity cases, including many international, maritime, securities, trade and interstate business disputes.
Complex Business Litigation

Florida's specialist commercial dockets are circuit-specific. Parties should not assume that a statewide business court exists. The case must be filed, transferred or assigned in a circuit that operates a Complex Business Litigation or Business Court division and meet the relevant local criteria.

Miami-Dade CountyThe Eleventh Judicial Circuit maintains Complex Business Litigation Sections within its Circuit Civil Division. The sections are dedicated to the orderly and efficient disposition of complex business matters.
Broward CountyThe Seventeenth Judicial Circuit operates a Complex Litigation Unit with business and tort subdivisions. Complex business cases can be assigned under local administrative orders and transfer procedures.
Orange and Osceola CountiesThe Ninth Judicial Circuit operates a Business Court for qualifying complex business matters, including defined corporate, transaction, commercial-code and business tort claims under local criteria.
Hillsborough CountyThe Thirteenth Judicial Circuit operates a Complex Business Litigation Division for qualifying commercial matters, including governance, securities, antitrust, derivative, franchise, trade secret and related disputes.
Local EligibilitySubject matter, amount in controversy, complexity, number of parties, related actions and local administrative order criteria can affect specialist-docket assignment.
Strategic ConsequenceVenue selection can influence not only the jury pool and local practice, but also whether a specialised business docket and dedicated case management are available.
Procedure

Florida state civil litigation is governed principally by the Florida Rules of Civil Procedure, the Florida Rules of Judicial Administration, statutes and local circuit rules or administrative orders. Specialist Complex Business Litigation sections apply local case-management protocols in addition to the general civil procedure framework.

Forum and VenueProper court and county venue depend on the parties, contract, place of performance, alleged conduct, property location, forum-selection clause and statutory venue rules.
CommencementA civil action is generally commenced by filing a complaint in the appropriate court and serving the summons and complaint on each defendant.
PleadingsThe complaint should state the claims, factual allegations and requested relief. Defendants respond by answer, motion to dismiss, motion to strike or other procedural motion as applicable.
DiscoveryFlorida permits extensive civil discovery, including document demands, interrogatories, requests for admissions, depositions, subpoenas, inspections and expert discovery.
Complex Business AssignmentTransfer or assignment to a Complex Business Litigation division is governed by local administrative orders and procedures. A party may need to seek transfer through the currently assigned circuit judge or designated complex-case process.
Case ManagementComplex business matters commonly involve active judicial management, discovery schedules, case-management conferences, motion practice, mediation and trial preparation orders.

Appeals

Florida state appeals generally proceed from circuit courts to the relevant District Court of Appeal. The Florida Supreme Court exercises discretionary review in selected cases. Federal court appeals proceed from the Florida federal district courts to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit.

State Court AppealFinal circuit court judgments and appealable orders are generally reviewed by the District Court of Appeal for the relevant appellate district.
Florida Supreme Court ReviewThe Florida Supreme Court may exercise discretionary or other constitutionally assigned review in selected state-law cases.
Federal AppealFederal district court judgments in Florida are generally appealed to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, insurance, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.

Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside merits analysis. The commercial value of a Florida judgment depends on asset availability, enforceability of the result and the practical route to collection, levy, garnishment, lien, receivership or compliance.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, insurance coverage, entity structure, security, banking relationships, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Florida ExecutionFlorida judgment enforcement may involve writs of execution, judgment liens, garnishment, levy, charging orders, receivership and other remedies under the applicable enforcement framework.
Interstate and Foreign DimensionOut-of-state and foreign judgment recognition, asset location, federal or state registration and applicable statutory rules can matter where the dispute extends beyond Florida.
Federal Court Interface

A Florida commercial dispute may also fall within federal court jurisdiction. Federal forum analysis should occur before filing and again when assessing removal after a state-court action is commenced.

Federal QuestionFederal district courts may hear claims arising under federal law, including federal securities, intellectual property, antitrust, bankruptcy-related, maritime, trade and other federal claims.
Diversity JurisdictionFederal jurisdiction may arise where parties are citizens of different states or countries and the statutory amount-in-controversy requirement is met, subject to statutory limitations.
Removal and RemandDefendants may remove qualifying state-court actions to federal court. Plaintiffs may seek remand where federal jurisdiction or removal procedure is defective.
Florida Federal DistrictsThe Northern, Middle and Southern Districts of Florida have distinct local rules, judges, divisions and commercial litigation environments. The Southern District is particularly significant for Miami-based international business disputes.
Strategic ConsequenceThe selected forum affects pleading standards, discovery, motion practice, jury dynamics, trial timing, appellate route and enforcement strategy.
Cross-Border Relevance

Florida is a major international trade, finance, real estate, hospitality, maritime, aviation and Latin America-facing commercial jurisdiction. Cross-border disputes frequently involve Florida entities, Miami-based finance and trade relationships, foreign investors, real estate assets and counterparties in Latin America and the Caribbean.

Forum SelectionCross-border contracts should assess Florida circuit-court clauses, local complex business docket availability, federal court implications, arbitration clauses, governing law, service arrangements, asset location and enforcement destination together.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the proper Florida court, service pathway, personal jurisdiction basis, complex business division eligibility, evidence, interim protection and enforcement destination early.
Miami-Dade RelevanceMiami-Dade's Complex Business Litigation Sections and the Southern District of Florida are significant forums for international business, trade, finance, real estate and Latin America-related disputes.
Real Estate and Trade ContextFlorida commercial cases often require early planning for real estate assets, maritime or logistics issues, insurance coverage, foreign ownership, banking relationships and cross-border enforcement.
Practical RiskA valid claim can still lose commercial value if venue, complex docket availability, state versus federal forum, discovery, interim relief or enforcement planning is not assessed early.

Forum RiskCircuit court, local complex business division, federal court and arbitration routes have different jurisdictional foundations, venue, procedure and appellate consequences.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in Florida?Most significant Florida state-law commercial disputes are filed in circuit courts. County courts hear lower-value civil matters within their jurisdiction. Appeals proceed to the District Courts of Appeal and, in selected matters, the Florida Supreme Court.
Does Florida Have a Statewide Business Court?Florida does not have a single statewide business court. Several judicial circuits operate specialised Complex Business Litigation or Business Court divisions within their circuit civil courts, including Miami-Dade, Broward, Orange and Osceola, and Hillsborough.
What Is the Miami-Dade Complex Business Litigation Division?The Eleventh Judicial Circuit’s Complex Business Litigation Sections are dedicated circuit civil sections designed to promote the orderly and efficient disposition of complex business matters in Miami-Dade County.
Can a Florida Commercial Case Be Moved to Federal Court?A Florida state-court action may be removable to federal court where federal subject-matter jurisdiction exists, commonly through a federal question or diversity of citizenship with the required amount in controversy, subject to statutory limits and procedure.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Florida?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Florida, but should assess circuit and county venue, complex business division availability, personal jurisdiction, federal removal exposure, discovery, interim relief, entity authority and enforcement planning from the outset.
