Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Texas is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, energy, infrastructure, technology, corporate relationships, banking, real estate, trade, construction, fiduciary duties and other commercial activity. Most Texas state-law commercial disputes are litigated in district courts, which are the principal trial courts of general jurisdiction.

Texas has added an important specialist forum: the Texas Business Court. Created by legislation and operational for cases commenced from 1 September 2024, it is a statewide specialised trial court designed to resolve certain complex business disputes. The Business Court has concurrent jurisdiction with district courts over defined corporate-governance, derivative, fiduciary-duty, securities, Business Organizations Code and high-value commercial matters.

For businesses, a commercially useful Texas litigation strategy requires early analysis of Business Court eligibility, the relevant division and venue, district-court alternatives, contract clauses, discovery, interim relief, energy or industry context, appeal route and federal court exposure. The Fifteenth Court of Appeals is a key part of the new Business Court structure for qualifying appeals.

Court Structure

Texas has a layered state court system. District courts are principal trial courts of general jurisdiction for major commercial matters. The Texas Business Court is a specialist statewide trial court for defined complex business disputes. Appeals proceed through the Texas Courts of Appeals, including the Fifteenth Court of Appeals for Business Court matters, and potentially the Supreme Court of Texas.

Texas District CourtsPrincipal trial courts of general jurisdiction. Most substantial Texas state-law commercial disputes, contracts, business torts and corporate claims are filed in district courts.
Texas Business CourtStatewide specialised trial court created to resolve specified complex business disputes. It has concurrent jurisdiction with district courts over qualifying matters under Texas Government Code Chapter 25A.
County Courts at Law and County CourtsMay hear civil business matters within their statutory jurisdiction, which varies across counties and should be distinguished from district-court and Business Court routes.
Texas Courts of AppealsIntermediate appellate courts that review district-court and other trial-court judgments within their geographic appellate districts.
Fifteenth Court of AppealsSpecialised statewide appellate court created with the Business Court structure. It hears appeals from the Texas Business Court and other matters assigned by statute.
Supreme Court of TexasTexas's highest court for civil and juvenile matters. It exercises discretionary review over qualifying civil appeals and important state-law questions.
Federal District CourtsFederal trial courts in the Northern, Southern, Eastern and Western Districts of Texas hear federal-question and qualifying diversity disputes, including many energy, securities, trade and interstate business matters.
Texas Business Court

The Texas Business Court is a specialist forum rather than a replacement for district courts. Its jurisdiction is statutory and concurrent in defined categories. Business Court eligibility must be checked carefully against Texas Government Code Chapter 25A, current Business Court divisions, procedural rules and the facts of the individual dispute.

Corporate and Governance MattersThe Business Court has jurisdiction over defined derivative proceedings, corporate-governance actions, fiduciary-duty claims, owner and manager disputes, certain securities claims and actions arising from the Business Organizations Code when statutory requirements are met.
Public Company MattersMatters involving publicly traded companies may fall within Business Court jurisdiction under the statutory framework, subject to the applicable conditions.
High-Value MattersSpecified categories generally require statutory amount-in-controversy thresholds. Certain governance and related matters use a lower threshold, while qualified transactions and other commercial matters may require a higher threshold.
Qualified TransactionsThe court may hear qualifying actions arising from transactions meeting the statutory definition and value threshold, including certain complex commercial transactions.
Contractual Business Court ClauseCertain high-value contract or commercial transaction disputes may be eligible where the parties agreed that the Business Court would have jurisdiction, subject to statutory conditions and exclusions.
Concurrent JurisdictionBusiness Court jurisdiction is concurrent with civil district courts in its defined categories; forum choice can therefore be strategic rather than automatic.
Operational StatusBusiness Court divisions and judicial appointments have been phased in. Current division availability, venue and filing rules should be confirmed before relying on the forum.
Procedure

Texas civil procedure is governed principally by the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure and the Texas Rules of Evidence, together with court-specific local rules. The Texas Rules of Civil Procedure apply to business courts, including the specialist Business Court rules adopted in 2024. Business Court proceedings are designed for sophisticated commercial matters and specialised judicial management.

Forum and VenueCorrect forum depends on Business Court statutory jurisdiction, division availability, venue, contract clauses, subject matter, parties, amount in controversy and any federal jurisdictional basis.
CommencementA Texas civil action is generally commenced by filing an original petition in the appropriate court and serving citation and petition on each defendant.
PleadingsThe petition should state the factual basis, legal claims, requested relief and jurisdictional allegations. Defendants respond by answer, special appearance, plea to the jurisdiction or other appropriate motion.
DiscoveryTexas civil discovery may include written discovery, document requests, admissions, depositions, subpoenas, inspections and expert discovery, subject to the applicable discovery-control plan and court orders.
Business Court ProcedureTexas Rules of Civil Procedure 352 to 359 and related rules address Business Court practice, including appearance, removal and remand, supplemental jurisdiction and procedure in the specialist court.
Case ManagementComplex business matters may involve tailored scheduling, discovery management, dispositive motion practice, expert proceedings, mediation and trial preparation under active judicial supervision.

Appeals

The appellate route depends on the court of first instance. District-court judgments generally proceed to the geographically relevant Texas Court of Appeals. Texas Business Court appeals generally proceed to the Fifteenth Court of Appeals. The Supreme Court of Texas exercises discretionary review over qualifying civil matters.

District Court AppealFinal district-court judgments and appealable orders generally proceed to the appropriate Texas Court of Appeals for the relevant appellate district.
Business Court AppealAppeals from Texas Business Court judgments generally proceed to the Fifteenth Court of Appeals, subject to statutory jurisdiction and appellate rules.
Supreme Court ReviewThe Supreme Court of Texas may grant discretionary review in selected civil cases involving important Texas-law issues.
Federal AppealFederal district court judgments in Texas are generally appealed to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.

Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside merits analysis. The commercial value of a Texas judgment depends on asset availability, enforceability of the result and the practical route to collection, levy, lien, turnover, receivership or compliance.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, insurance coverage, entity structure, security, bank exposure, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Texas ExecutionTexas enforcement may involve writs of execution, judgment liens, turnover orders, garnishment, receivership and other remedies under applicable Texas law.
Interstate and Foreign DimensionOut-of-state and foreign judgment recognition, asset location, federal or state registration and applicable statutory rules can matter where the dispute extends beyond Texas.
Federal Court Interface

A Texas commercial dispute may also fall within federal court jurisdiction. Federal forum analysis should occur before filing and again when assessing removal after a state-court action is commenced.

Federal QuestionFederal district courts may hear claims arising under federal law, including federal securities, intellectual property, antitrust, bankruptcy-related, energy-regulatory, trade and other federal claims.
Diversity JurisdictionFederal jurisdiction may arise where parties are citizens of different states or countries and the statutory amount-in-controversy requirement is met, subject to statutory limitations.
Removal and RemandDefendants may remove qualifying state-court actions to federal court. Plaintiffs may seek remand where federal jurisdiction or removal procedure is defective.
Texas Federal DistrictsThe Northern, Southern, Eastern and Western Districts of Texas have distinct local rules, judges, divisions and commercial litigation environments.
Strategic ConsequenceThe selected forum affects pleading standards, discovery, motion practice, jury dynamics, trial timing, appellate route and enforcement strategy.
Cross-Border Relevance

Texas is a major energy, infrastructure, trade, manufacturing, technology and cross-border business jurisdiction. International commercial disputes frequently involve Texas entities, projects, commodities, supply chains, energy assets, Mexican and Latin American connections, and multi-state or foreign counterparties.

Forum SelectionCross-border contracts should assess Texas district-court or Business Court clauses, federal court implications, arbitration clauses, governing law, service arrangements, asset location and enforcement destination together.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the proper Texas court, service pathway, personal jurisdiction basis, Business Court eligibility, evidence, interim protection and enforcement destination early.
Business Court InterfaceCross-border corporate, governance, fiduciary, securities and high-value transaction disputes may be candidates for Business Court treatment when statutory eligibility and venue requirements are met.
Energy and Project RelevanceEnergy, infrastructure, construction, joint venture, supply-chain and industrial disputes often require early contract, asset, insurance and expert-evidence planning.
Practical RiskA valid claim can still lose commercial value if Business Court eligibility, state versus federal forum, discovery, interim relief or enforcement planning is not assessed early.
Operating Constraints & Risks
Forum RiskDistrict court, Business Court, federal court and arbitration routes have different jurisdictional foundations, venue, procedure and appellate consequences.
Business Court RiskBusiness Court jurisdiction is statutory and limited. Subject matter, amount, parties, venue, division availability and exclusions must be confirmed before filing or removal.
Discovery RiskTexas commercial litigation can involve extensive document, ESI, deposition and expert discovery, requiring early preservation and proportionality planning.
Interim-Relief RiskDelay may reduce the practical availability or value of injunction, attachment, receivership and other urgent protective relief.
Recovery RiskA favourable decision may have limited commercial value where assets, insurance, entity structure or judgment enforcement have not been assessed.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in Texas?Most Texas state-law commercial disputes are filed in state district courts. Certain complex business disputes may fall within the concurrent jurisdiction of the Texas Business Court, while federal courts hear matters with federal-question or diversity jurisdiction.
What Is the Texas Business Court?The Texas Business Court is a statewide specialised trial court created to resolve certain complex business disputes. It has concurrent jurisdiction with district courts over qualifying corporate-governance, fiduciary-duty, securities, Business Organizations Code and high-value commercial matters.
When Can a Case Be Filed in the Texas Business Court?Jurisdiction depends on statutory subject matter, amount in controversy, parties and venue. The court has defined categories involving publicly traded companies, qualifying corporate-governance and fiduciary matters, and certain higher-value transactions or contracts, subject to Texas Government Code Chapter 25A and the Business Court's rules.
What Court Hears Appeals from the Texas Business Court?Appeals from the Texas Business Court generally proceed to the Fifteenth Court of Appeals, subject to statutory jurisdiction and applicable appellate rules.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Texas?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Texas, but should assess Business Court eligibility, district-court venue, personal jurisdiction, federal removal exposure, discovery, interim relief, entity authority and enforcement planning from the outset.
