Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Illinois is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, commodities, trade, transportation, banking, insurance, manufacturing, corporate relationships, real estate, construction and other commercial activity. Most significant Illinois state-law commercial disputes are filed in the circuit court of the relevant county.

Cook County, which includes Chicago, offers an important specialist business docket: the Commercial Calendar Section of the Circuit Court of Cook County's Law Division. Commercial Calendars hear cases based on contract, tort or other theories when the dispute involves a commercial relationship between the parties. The Section applies a Commercial Calendar Standing Order and individual commercial calendars managed by designated judges.

For businesses, a commercially useful Illinois litigation strategy requires early analysis of circuit and county venue, Commercial Calendar eligibility, Illinois Code of Civil Procedure, discovery, interim relief, appellate exposure and federal court removal or filing options. Chicago's role in finance, commodities, transport, insurance and interstate trade makes federal and cross-border dimensions especially relevant.

Court Structure

Illinois has a unified state court system comprising circuit courts, an Appellate Court and the Illinois Supreme Court. Circuit courts are the principal trial courts for commercial matters. Cook County is the First Judicial District and a major specialist commercial litigation forum.

Illinois Circuit CourtsTrial courts of general jurisdiction in Illinois's judicial circuits. Major state-law commercial disputes, contracts, business torts, corporate claims and construction disputes are generally filed here.
Circuit Court of Cook CountyIllinois's largest trial court, serving Chicago and Cook County. Its Law Division includes the Commercial Calendar Section for qualifying commercial cases.
Commercial Calendar SectionSpecialist section within the Cook County Law Division. It hears commercial cases based on contract, tort or other theories involving a commercial relationship between the parties.
Illinois Appellate CourtIntermediate appellate court divided into five judicial districts. It reviews final circuit court judgments and appealable orders.
Illinois Supreme CourtIllinois's highest state court, exercising discretionary review over selected significant state-law questions and other matters assigned by law.
Federal District CourtsFederal trial courts in the Northern, Central and Southern Districts of Illinois hear federal-question and qualifying diversity cases, including many finance, commodities, trade, securities and interstate business disputes.
Commercial Calendar

The Cook County Commercial Calendar Section is a specialist litigation environment within the Law Division, rather than a separate statewide business court. It is particularly relevant to Chicago-based and major Illinois commercial cases with substantial contract, tort, finance, trade, corporate or complex business elements.

Commercial Relationship TestCommercial Calendars hear cases, whether based on contract, tort or another theory, that involve a commercial relationship between the parties.
Law Division LocationThe Commercial Calendar Section operates within the Law Division of the Circuit Court of Cook County.
Individual CalendarsCases are assigned to individual Commercial Calendar judges, supporting commercial-case familiarity, active supervision and standing-order practice.
Standing OrderThe Commercial Calendar Standing Order sets procedural expectations for commercial cases and should be reviewed alongside the Illinois Code of Civil Procedure, Illinois Supreme Court Rules and applicable local rules.
Case ManagementCommercial Calendar practice is designed to facilitate focused commercial case management, including discovery, motions, status conferences and trial preparation.
Venue ImportanceCook County filing can affect not only venue and jury considerations but also access to the Commercial Calendar Section and its specialist commercial procedures.
Procedure

Illinois state civil litigation is governed principally by the Illinois Code of Civil Procedure, Illinois Supreme Court Rules and applicable circuit court rules. Cook County Commercial Calendar cases are additionally managed under the Commercial Calendar Standing Order and Law Division practice. The procedural environment includes pleadings, discovery, motion practice, case-management conferences, mediation and trial preparation.

Forum and VenueProper court and county venue depend on the parties, transaction, contract forum clause, place of performance, alleged conduct, property location and statutory venue rules.
Commercial Calendar EligibilityCommercial Calendar assignment depends on the commercial relationship between the parties, case type, Cook County venue and applicable Law Division assignment practice.
CommencementA civil action is generally commenced by filing a complaint and paying the required filing fee, followed by issuance and service of summons on each defendant.
PleadingsThe complaint should set out the claims, factual allegations and requested relief. Defendants generally respond by answer, motion to dismiss or other appropriate procedural motion.
DiscoveryIllinois permits civil discovery including document demands, interrogatories, requests for admissions, depositions, subpoenas, inspections, electronically stored information and expert discovery.
Case ManagementIllinois Supreme Court Rule 218 and local Commercial Calendar practice provide for case-management conferences, discovery planning, motion schedules, settlement discussion and trial preparation.

Appeals

Illinois state appeals generally proceed from circuit courts to the Illinois Appellate Court. The Illinois Supreme Court exercises discretionary review in selected cases. Federal court appeals proceed from Illinois federal district courts to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit.

State Court AppealFinal circuit court judgments and appealable orders are generally reviewed by the Illinois Appellate Court in the relevant judicial district.
Illinois Supreme Court ReviewThe Illinois Supreme Court may grant discretionary review in selected cases involving important state-law questions.
Federal AppealFederal district court judgments in Illinois are generally appealed to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, insurance, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.

Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside merits analysis. The commercial value of an Illinois judgment depends on asset availability, enforceability of the result and the practical route to collection, levy, lien, turnover, receivership or compliance.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, insurance coverage, entity structure, security, banking relationships, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Illinois ExecutionIllinois judgment enforcement may involve citations to discover assets, garnishment, judgment liens, levies, turnover orders, receivership and other remedies under the applicable enforcement framework.
Interstate and Foreign DimensionOut-of-state and foreign judgment recognition, asset location, federal or state registration and applicable statutory rules can matter where the dispute extends beyond Illinois.
Federal Court Interface

An Illinois commercial dispute may also fall within federal court jurisdiction. Federal forum analysis should occur before filing and again when assessing removal after a state-court action is commenced.

Federal QuestionFederal district courts may hear claims arising under federal law, including federal securities, intellectual property, antitrust, bankruptcy-related, commodities, trade and other federal claims.
Diversity JurisdictionFederal jurisdiction may arise where parties are citizens of different states or countries and the statutory amount-in-controversy requirement is met, subject to statutory limitations.
Removal and RemandDefendants may remove qualifying state-court actions to federal court. Plaintiffs may seek remand where federal jurisdiction or removal procedure is defective.
Northern District of IllinoisThe U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois is a major Chicago-based federal commercial forum for finance, commodities, securities, trade, transport, class and interstate business litigation.
Strategic ConsequenceThe selected forum affects pleading standards, discovery, motion practice, jury dynamics, trial timing, appellate route and enforcement strategy.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in Illinois?Most Illinois state-law commercial disputes are filed in the circuit court of the relevant county. In Cook County, qualifying commercial matters may be assigned to the Commercial Calendar Section of the Law Division. Appeals proceed to the Illinois Appellate Court and, in selected matters, the Illinois Supreme Court.
What Is the Cook County Commercial Calendar Section?The Commercial Calendar Section is a specialised part of the Cook County Circuit Court Law Division. It hears cases based on contract, tort or other theories that involve a commercial relationship between the parties and applies a Commercial Calendar Standing Order.
What Law Governs Illinois Commercial Litigation Procedure?The Illinois Code of Civil Procedure, Illinois Supreme Court Rules and applicable circuit court rules govern state civil litigation. Cook County Commercial Calendar matters are also subject to their standing order and Law Division practice.
Can an Illinois Commercial Case Be Moved to Federal Court?A state-court commercial action may be removable to federal court if federal subject-matter jurisdiction exists, commonly through a federal question or diversity of citizenship with the required amount in controversy, subject to statutory limits and procedure.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Illinois?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Illinois, but should assess circuit and county venue, Commercial Calendar availability, personal jurisdiction, federal removal exposure, discovery, interim relief, entity authority and enforcement planning from the outset.
