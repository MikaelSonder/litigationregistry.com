Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Croatia is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, payment obligations, company relationships, trade, maritime and aviation activity, insolvency-linked matters or other commercial relationships. Croatia has specialised commercial courts at first instance and a High Commercial Court at second instance.

The Civil Procedure Act supplies the main procedural framework. Its rules on procedure before commercial courts apply to disputes falling within commercial court competence unless a special procedure is prescribed. Commercial courts also have functions related to company registers, commercial registers, insolvency matters and recognition of foreign court rulings and arbitration awards in commercial disputes.

For businesses, a commercially useful Croatian litigation strategy requires early analysis of commercial court competence, e-communication requirements, documentation, interim protection, appeal exposure and whether a successful decision can be converted into payment, compliance or recovery.

Court Structure

Croatia has a specialised commercial court structure. Commercial courts act as first-instance specialised courts, and the High Commercial Court of the Republic of Croatia acts as the specialist second-instance court for commercial-court decisions.

Commercial CourtsSpecialised first-instance courts that hear contentious and non-contentious matters within their statutory competence, including commercial disputes and related company, register and insolvency matters.
High Commercial CourtSpecialised appellate court that hears appeals against first-instance rulings of commercial courts, resolves certain territorial-jurisdiction disputes and deals with delegation of commercial court jurisdiction.
Supreme Court of the Republic of CroatiaHighest court in the ordinary judicial system; may be relevant where statutory review or extraordinary remedies are available.
Commercial Court FunctionsCommercial courts maintain court registers, handle defined registration matters, conduct insolvency-related procedures, secure evidence and carry out recognition procedures for foreign rulings and arbitration awards in commercial disputes.
SpecialisationThe specialised commercial court track distinguishes Croatia from systems where commercial disputes are handled only through general civil courts.
Procedure

The Civil Procedure Act governs the procedural rules under which courts decide commercial and other civil-law disputes. In proceedings before commercial courts, the Act’s commercial-court rules apply where no different special procedure is prescribed.

Forum and CompetenceCommercial court competence should be assessed by the type of dispute, party status, statutory subject matter and territorial connection.
CommencementProceedings commence through the appropriate filing with the competent commercial court, subject to the applicable procedural route and electronic communication requirements.
Pleadings and Case FramingThe claim and defence should establish the factual basis, legal grounds, relief sought and supporting evidence in a coherent court-ready form.
Electronic CommunicationLegal entities seated in Croatia generally use e-Komunikacija for communication with courts in civil and commercial proceedings, subject to applicable rules and technical requirements.
MediationMediation can be used in commercial disputes before regular and specialised courts at all stages, including during appeal proceedings.

Appeals

The High Commercial Court of the Republic of Croatia hears appeals against first-instance rulings of commercial courts. It also resolves certain competence questions among commercial courts. The Supreme Court may become relevant through statutory review or extraordinary remedies.

Commercial AppealAppeals from commercial court decisions proceed to the High Commercial Court under the applicable procedural rules.
Jurisdiction QuestionsThe High Commercial Court resolves disputes concerning territorial jurisdiction among commercial courts and may rule on delegation of jurisdiction.
Supreme ReviewThe Supreme Court may be relevant to the extent a statutory review or extraordinary remedy is available.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.

Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside the merits analysis. The commercial value of a Croatian judgment depends on the availability of assets, the enforceability of the result and the practical route to payment or compliance.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, security, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Foreign Decisions and AwardsCommercial courts have functions in recognition procedures for foreign court rulings and arbitration awards in commercial disputes.
Cross-Border DimensionEU rules and applicable international instruments can matter where the judgment, debtor, assets or relevant business activity extends beyond Croatia.
Cross-Border Relevance

Croatia is an EU Member State. Cross-border commercial litigation may require analysis of EU rules on jurisdiction, service, evidence and recognition or enforcement, alongside Croatian commercial court competence and domestic procedure.

Forum SelectionCross-border cases should assess contractual jurisdiction clauses, the defendant’s establishment, place of performance, applicable EU rules and Croatian commercial court competence.
European Union InterfaceEU judicial-cooperation rules can be central to civil and commercial disputes involving parties, evidence, assets or judgments in different Member States.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the proper commercial court, service pathway, translation and document requirements, electronic communication position, potential interim protection and enforcement destination early.
Practical RiskA valid claim can still lose commercial value if it is filed in the wrong forum, poorly documented or unsupported by an enforcement plan.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in Croatia?Commercial courts are specialised first-instance courts for matters within their statutory competence. The High Commercial Court of the Republic of Croatia hears appeals from first-instance commercial court decisions.
What Is the Main Procedural Framework for Croatian Commercial Litigation?The Civil Procedure Act governs civil procedure. Its commercial-court provisions apply to disputes within the competence of commercial courts unless special procedure rules provide otherwise.
Are Croatian Companies Required to Communicate Electronically with Commercial Courts?Legal entities seated in Croatia are generally required to use electronic communication with courts in civil and commercial proceedings through the e-Komunikacija system, subject to applicable rules.
Can Commercial Courts Recognise Foreign Judgments and Arbitration Awards?Commercial courts have statutory functions that include procedures for recognition of foreign court rulings and arbitration awards in commercial disputes.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Croatia?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Croatia, but should assess commercial court competence, service, evidence, electronic communication, translation, timing and enforcement planning from the outset.
