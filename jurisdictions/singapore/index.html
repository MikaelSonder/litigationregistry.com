Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Singapore is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, finance, trade, shareholder relationships, technology, supply chains, insolvency-related activity and other commercial transactions. Singapore combines a domestic civil-litigation system with a dedicated international commercial court route for qualifying cross-border disputes.

The Supreme Court comprises the General Division of the High Court, the Appellate Division and the Court of Appeal. The State Courts handle matters within their civil jurisdiction. The Singapore International Commercial Court (SICC) is a division of the General Division of the High Court and part of the Supreme Court; it is designed to hear transnational disputes that are international and commercial in nature, including disputes submitted through written jurisdiction agreements and cases transferred from the High Court.

The Rules of Court 2021 apply generally to civil proceedings commenced on or after 1 April 2022 in the Supreme Court and State Courts. They emphasise justice, efficiency, proportionality, amicable resolution and active case management. For businesses, a commercially useful litigation strategy requires early forum analysis, documentary preparation, costs control, interim-protection planning and a clear recovery path.

Court Structure

Singapore has a structured civil court system comprising the State Courts and the Supreme Court. The Supreme Court includes the General Division of the High Court, the Appellate Division and the Court of Appeal. The SICC sits within the General Division as a specialist route for international and commercial disputes.

State CourtsHear civil matters within their statutory jurisdiction, including business disputes that do not fall within the original jurisdiction or specialist route of the Supreme Court.
General Division of the High CourtPrincipal superior first-instance court for civil and commercial matters within its jurisdiction, including complex and high-value disputes.
Singapore International Commercial CourtA division of the General Division designed to hear international and commercial disputes, including cases submitted by written jurisdiction agreement or transferred from the High Court.
Appellate DivisionHears defined civil appeals from the General Division and other matters allocated to it under the applicable judicial structure.
Court of AppealSingapore’s apex appellate court for matters allocated to it, including appeals from SICC proceedings and other Supreme Court matters as provided by law.
SICC CharacterFor a qualifying SICC action, the dispute must be international and commercial in nature and satisfy the applicable jurisdictional conditions. A written SICC jurisdiction agreement is a central route into the court.
Procedure

The Rules of Court 2021 (ROC 2021) generally govern civil proceedings commenced on or after 1 April 2022 in the Supreme Court and State Courts. Separate SICC Rules 2021 apply to SICC proceedings. The ROC 2021 framework is built around efficient, proportionate and cost-conscious justice, supported by active court case management and a duty to consider amicable resolution.

Forum and CompetenceCorrect forum depends on claim value, complexity, subject matter, statutory jurisdiction, contractual clauses and whether the dispute qualifies for SICC jurisdiction.
CommencementCivil proceedings are commenced through the applicable originating process and filing route under the ROC 2021 or, for SICC matters, the SICC Rules 2021.
Amicable ResolutionParties have a duty to consider amicable resolution before commencement and during proceedings, consistent with the ROC 2021 approach.
Case ManagementThe court actively manages proceedings to identify issues, control evidence and costs, promote procedural efficiency and resolve the dispute justly.
SICC RouteThe SICC can hear qualifying international and commercial claims where the parties submit by written jurisdiction agreement or where a High Court case is transferred to the SICC.
Representation and LanguageSingapore litigation is conducted in English. Representation and foreign-law issues should be assessed early, particularly in international commercial matters.
Process Flow
1. Dispute AssessmentIdentify the commercial conflict, legal position, business objective and likely counterparty response.
2. Forum AnalysisDetermine whether the dispute belongs in the State Courts, General Division, SICC, arbitration or another dispute-resolution route.
3. Evidence and Cost ReviewOrganise contracts, correspondence, financial records, witness evidence, technical material and a proportionate litigation budget.
4. Pre-Action ResolutionAssess amicable-resolution options and preserve a clear record of the parties’ engagement with resolution possibilities.
5. Commencement and Case ManagementFile the appropriate originating process, manage pleadings, discovery or evidence issues, directions and procedural milestones.
6. Judgment and AppealAssess the result, appellate route and whether appeal is legally and commercially justified.
7. EnforcementTranslate the result into payment, asset recovery, compliance or settlement leverage.
Appeals

The appellate route depends on the originating court and the type of decision. Singapore’s appellate structure includes the Appellate Division and the Court of Appeal. Appeals from SICC decisions are heard by the Court of Appeal, subject to the applicable rules and permission requirements where relevant.

State Court AppealsAppeals from State Court decisions follow the allocated appellate route under the applicable legislation and ROC 2021 framework.
General Division AppealsDefined civil appeals from the General Division may proceed to the Appellate Division or Court of Appeal depending on the matter and statutory allocation.
SICC AppealsAppeals from SICC decisions are heard by the Court of Appeal in accordance with the SICC Rules and applicable Supreme Court framework.
Commercial DecisionAppeal should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.

Witness and Expert EvidenceWitness, technical, accounting, valuation or foreign-law evidence may be needed depending on the dispute and the issues in proof.
ProportionalityUnder ROC 2021, the parties and court should approach evidence and procedural steps with attention to efficiency, proportionality and costs.
Interim ProtectionUrgent protective measures may be relevant where delay risks assets, evidence, contractual rights or effective recovery.

Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside merits analysis. The commercial value of a Singapore judgment depends on asset availability, enforceability of the result and the practical route to payment or compliance in Singapore or abroad.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, security, group structure, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
International DimensionSICC judgments and Singapore court judgments may have particular cross-border relevance, but recognition and enforcement depend on the destination jurisdiction and applicable legal framework.
Business Hub ContextCross-border corporate, finance, trade and holding-company structures make early asset and entity analysis commercially important.
Cross-Border Relevance

Singapore is a major international business and dispute-resolution centre. The SICC makes Singapore particularly relevant where parties seek a court-based forum for disputes that are international and commercial in nature.

SICC JurisdictionThe SICC generally hears an action that is international and commercial in nature and where the parties have submitted to its jurisdiction under a written jurisdiction agreement, subject to the applicable rules.
Transfer RouteThe SICC may hear cases transferred from the Singapore High Court where the applicable transfer conditions are met.
Foreign PartiesThe SICC does not decline jurisdiction solely because a dispute is connected to another jurisdiction where the parties have a written SICC jurisdiction agreement.
Forum SelectionCross-border contracts should assess SICC clauses, Singapore High Court clauses, arbitration clauses, governing law, service arrangements, asset location and enforcement destination together.
Practical RiskA jurisdiction clause is only commercially useful when it aligns with the contract, the parties, evidence, potential relief and the location of assets or counterparties.
Operating Constraints & Risks
Forum RiskState Court, General Division, SICC and arbitration routes have different competence, procedural and strategic implications.
Jurisdiction-Clause RiskInternational commercial clauses should be drafted and assessed carefully to ensure the intended court has jurisdiction and the clause supports the commercial objective.
Evidence and Cost RiskWeak documentation, poor early evidence planning or disproportionate procedure can reduce leverage and increase cost exposure.
Amicable-Resolution RiskParties should consider and document reasonable amicable-resolution engagement in light of the ROC 2021 framework.
Recovery RiskA favourable decision may have limited commercial value where assets are unavailable or enforcement has not been planned.

FAQ
Which Courts Hear Commercial Disputes in Singapore?Commercial disputes may be heard in the State Courts or the General Division of the High Court, depending on the claim and applicable jurisdiction. International commercial disputes may be heard by the Singapore International Commercial Court, a division of the General Division of the High Court.
What Is the Singapore International Commercial Court?The SICC is a division of the General Division of the Singapore High Court and part of the Supreme Court of Singapore. It is designed to hear transnational disputes of an international and commercial nature, including cases submitted through written jurisdiction agreements or transferred from the High Court.
What Rules Govern Singapore Commercial Litigation?The Rules of Court 2021 generally apply to civil proceedings commenced on or after 1 April 2022 in the Supreme Court and State Courts, while SICC matters are governed by the SICC Rules 2021.
What Is Distinctive About the Rules of Court 2021?The Rules of Court 2021 emphasise the ideal of justice, efficient and proportionate resolution, the parties’ duty to consider amicable resolution, and active court case management.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Singapore?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Singapore and may use the SICC where jurisdictional conditions are met. They should assess forum, jurisdiction clauses, service, evidence, costs, foreign-law issues and enforcement planning from the outset.
