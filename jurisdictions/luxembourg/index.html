Commercial disputes in Luxembourg are generally handled by the District Courts sitting in commercial composition. Effective case planning depends on correct court competence, procedure choice, language, documentary evidence, representation requirements and enforcement readiness.

Luxembourg is an EU Member State and an international financial and corporate centre. Cross-border commercial disputes often require early analysis of forum clauses, service, evidence, recognition or enforcement and the location of assets or corporate entities.

Executive Summary

Commercial litigation in Luxembourg is the court-based process through which businesses pursue, defend or manage disputes arising from contracts, financial activity, company and shareholder relationships, insolvency, commercial leases, trade and other professional transactions. The primary procedural framework is the New Code of Civil Procedure (Nouveau Code de Procédure Civile).

Luxembourg does not operate independent commercial courts. The District Courts, located in Luxembourg City and Diekirch, have general jurisdiction in civil and commercial matters not assigned to another court. They sit in commercial composition for commercial cases, including commercial contracts, corporate disputes, insolvency and reorganisation matters. Magistrates’ Courts handle assigned lower-value civil and commercial matters and specified subject areas.

A distinctive feature of Luxembourg commercial litigation is that the claimant may generally choose an oral commercial procedure or a written civil procedure. This procedural choice, together with language, representation, cross-border entity structure and enforcement planning, can materially affect the conduct and commercial value of a case.

Court Structure

Luxembourg’s judicial courts hear civil, commercial and criminal cases. The civil and commercial hierarchy includes Magistrates’ Courts, District Courts, the Court of Appeal and the Court of Cassation.

Magistrates’ CourtsHandle certain lower-value civil and business matters, including payment orders, attachment orders, leases and civil or commercial claims within the applicable monetary jurisdiction.
District CourtsCourts of general jurisdiction for civil and commercial matters not expressly assigned elsewhere. Two District Courts operate in Luxembourg City and Diekirch.
Commercial ChambersCommercial courts are not independent bodies; commercial chambers or commercial compositions are embedded within the District Courts and hear commercial disputes, corporate disputes, insolvency and related matters.
Court of AppealHears appeals from eligible District Court decisions and from Magistrates’ Court decisions where appeal is available.
Court of CassationReviews decisions on points of law through cassation rather than conducting a new full review of factual merits.
Procedure

The New Code of Civil Procedure provides the main procedural framework. Commercial proceedings are generally initiated by service of a writ of summons or citation through a bailiff. In commercial matters, the claimant may generally choose between an oral commercial procedure and a written civil procedure, subject to the applicable competence and procedural rules.

Forum and CompetenceDistrict Courts have general commercial jurisdiction unless a matter is allocated to another court by subject matter or amount. Magistrates’ Courts have jurisdiction in assigned lower-value business matters and certain defined categories.
CommencementProceedings are commonly commenced by bailiff service of a writ of summons or citation on the defendant.
Procedural RouteCommercial claimants may generally choose an oral commercial route or a written civil route. The choice should be assessed with the claim type, evidence, representation, speed and strategic objective in mind.
RepresentationRepresentation requirements depend on the court and procedure. Representation by an avocat à la Cour is generally compulsory before the District Court in written civil procedure.
LanguagesLuxembourgish, French and German are used in the judicial environment. In practice, commercial litigation is commonly conducted in French; foreign-language documents and submissions may require suitable translation or procedural handling.
Written RecordCommercial cases often rely heavily on written pleadings and documentary evidence even where the procedural route is described as oral.

Appeals & Cassation

The appeal route depends on the court of first instance, the amount in dispute and the nature of the decision. The Court of Appeal reviews eligible judgments, while the Court of Cassation addresses points of law rather than conducting a new factual merits trial.

Magistrates’ Court AppealsAppeal availability depends on the statutory monetary and subject-matter rules. Decisions below the final-instance threshold are not appealable.
District Court AppealsEligible District Court decisions may be appealed to the Court of Appeal under the applicable rules.
Cassation ReviewThe Court of Cassation examines legal grounds and procedural correctness rather than reassessing the complete factual merits of the case.
Commercial DecisionAppeal and cassation should be assessed by legal grounds, timing, cost, enforcement impact and settlement leverage.

Enforcement

Enforcement planning should run alongside the merits analysis. In Luxembourg, the practical value of a judgment often depends on understanding asset location, corporate structure, security interests, cross-border recognition and the appropriate enforcement route.

Enforcement ObjectiveConvert an enforceable title into practical payment, asset recovery, compliance or negotiated resolution.
Commercial PlanningAsset intelligence, debtor profile, group structure, security, timing and likely enforcement routes should be assessed before final judgment where possible.
Cross-Border DimensionEU rules and applicable international instruments can be especially significant where assets, entities, funds or counterparties sit in different jurisdictions.
Financial Centre ContextCorporate, fund, finance and holding-company structures can make early enforcement and entity analysis commercially important.
Cross-Border Relevance

Luxembourg is an EU Member State and a significant international corporate and financial centre. Cross-border commercial litigation frequently involves forum-selection clauses, foreign parties, corporate structures, financial assets and recognition or enforcement in other EU or non-EU jurisdictions.

Forum SelectionCross-border cases should assess contractual jurisdiction clauses, the defendant’s establishment, place of performance, applicable EU rules and Luxembourg court competence.
European Union InterfaceEU judicial-cooperation rules can be central to civil and commercial disputes involving parties, evidence, assets or judgments in different Member States.
Foreign CompaniesForeign businesses should identify the proper court, procedure route, service pathway, language, representation and enforcement destination early.
Practical RiskA valid claim can still lose commercial value if it is filed in the wrong forum, poorly documented or unsupported by an enforcement plan.
Operating Constraints & Risks

Recovery RiskA favourable decision may have limited commercial value where assets are unavailable or enforcement has not been planned.

FAQ
Which Court Hears Commercial Disputes in Luxembourg?The District Court (Tribunal d’arrondissement) is the court of general jurisdiction for commercial matters unless the matter is expressly assigned elsewhere. Commercial cases are heard by the District Court sitting in commercial composition.
Does Luxembourg Have Independent Commercial Courts?No. Luxembourg does not have separate standalone commercial courts. Commercial chambers or commercial compositions are embedded within the District Courts.
What Is the Key Procedural Framework?Civil and commercial proceedings are governed principally by the New Code of Civil Procedure (Nouveau Code de Procédure Civile).
Can a Commercial Case Be Brought Orally in Luxembourg?In commercial matters, the claimant may generally choose an oral commercial procedure or a written civil procedure, subject to the court’s competence and applicable procedural rules.
Can Foreign Companies Litigate in Luxembourg?Yes. Foreign companies can litigate in Luxembourg, but should assess forum, procedure choice, service, language, representation, evidence, group structure and enforcement planning from the outset.
